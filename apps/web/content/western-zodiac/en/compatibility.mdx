## What the aspects mean

Count the steps from one sign to the other along the shorter route, with zero steps for the same sign. The wheel gives seven possible spacings. Signs four apart share an element and form a trine, conventionally grouped with the harmonious aspects. Signs two apart pair fire with air or earth with water and form a sextile, read as friendly and cooperative. Signs three apart share a modality but not an element; that square is described as dynamic, a source of friction that can also drive effort. Signs six apart sit opposite each other, an opposition, often read as attraction between complementary ends of one theme.

In Tetrabiblos I.13 and I.16, Ptolemy lists four aspects between distinct signs and treats separations of one or five signs as disjunct. Many modern astrologers call those spacings the 30-degree semi-sextile and the 150-degree quincunx, also known as the inconjunct, and group them as minor aspects. Both spacings link signs with different elements and modalities. A pair of the same sign repeats one element, modality and ruler, which tradition reads as recognition with a tendency to amplify.

## How to read the chart

Each row is one sign and each column is another; the cell where they cross shows an aspect label or a dash and links to a page about that pair. Shaded cells mark the harmonious aspects, outlined cells the challenging ones, and plain cells the same-sign and minor pairings. Reading along a row shows how one sign relates to the whole zodiac, which is the same view each profile gives in its own table, for example [Leo compatibility](/zodiac-signs/leo).

Every pair page then goes further than the grid: the two elements and modalities, the ruling planets, the rhythm the pair tends to share, and the points where tradition expects friction. [Aries and Leo](/zodiac-compatibility/aries-and-leo) is one example of a trine between two fire signs.

A useful way to explore is to pick one row and compare cells with the same marking. Two outlined cells in the Aries row, for instance, are Cancer and Capricorn, both squares; the pages for those pairs describe different elements meeting at the same angle, so the friction they describe takes a different shape. Comparing like with like shows how much of a reading comes from the aspect and how much from the particular signs involved.

## Beyond sun signs

The chart only compares the signs the Sun occupied at two births. An astrologer comparing two people would also look at the Moon, the rising sign, Venus and Mars, and at exact aspects between planets measured in degrees, which can point in a very different direction from the sun signs. For the basics of each sign, start from the [zodiac signs](/zodiac-signs) overview.

Read the grid as a map of traditional associations. If a description matches something you recognize, it may be a useful prompt for conversation; if it does not, that is just as informative. It does not predict how any relationship will unfold.
