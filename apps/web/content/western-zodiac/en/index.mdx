## How the twelve signs are ordered

The sequence begins with Aries at the March equinox, when the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north. From there each sign spans one twelfth of the ecliptic, the apparent path of the Sun against the background stars: Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and finally Pisces, which closes the circle just before the next equinox.

Because the signs are tied to the seasons rather than to the constellations, the order also tells a seasonal story in the Northern Hemisphere. Aries, Cancer, Libra and Capricorn each open a season. The signs in the middle of each season, such as Taurus or Scorpio, are associated with steadiness, and the signs that close a season, such as Gemini or Pisces, with change. Much of the traditional symbolism, from the ram of early spring to the sea-goat of midwinter, grew out of this calendar.

Opposite signs sit six places apart: Aries faces Libra, Taurus faces Scorpio, and so on around the wheel. Many readings pair a sign with its opposite to show two ends of one theme, such as independence and partnership.

## Elements and modalities

Each sign belongs to one of four elements, a scheme inherited from Greek natural philosophy. Fire takes in Aries, Leo and Sagittarius; earth takes in Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn; air takes in Gemini, Libra and Aquarius; and water takes in Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces. Tradition links fire with enthusiasm, earth with practicality, air with ideas and exchange, and water with feeling and memory.

Running across the elements are three modalities. Cardinal signs begin each season and are read as initiating. Fixed signs sit in the middle and are read as sustaining. Mutable signs end each season and are read as adapting. Every sign is a unique combination: Aries is the cardinal fire sign, Taurus the fixed earth sign, Gemini the mutable air sign, and the pattern repeats until each element has appeared once in each modality.

These two labels are the backbone of most comparisons between signs. Signs of the same element sit four places apart, while signs of the same modality sit three or six places apart, which is why the elements and modalities reappear when astrologers describe how two signs relate. ## Ruling planets

Classical astrology assigned each sign a ruling planet from the seven bodies visible to the naked eye. The Sun rules Leo and the Moon rules Cancer. Mercury rules Gemini and Virgo, Venus rules Taurus and Libra, Mars rules Aries and Scorpio, Jupiter rules Sagittarius and Pisces, and Saturn rules Capricorn and Aquarius. The arrangement is symmetrical: moving outward from Cancer and Leo, each planet takes one sign on either side in order of its distance from the Sun.

After Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were discovered, many modern astrologers gave them co-rulership of Aquarius, Pisces and Scorpio. This site lists the traditional ruler first and the modern one beside it where it applies. In a reading, the ruler adds a theme to the sign: Mars is associated with drive, Venus with attraction and value, Saturn with structure and time. Two signs that share a ruler, such as Gemini and Virgo under Mercury, are often described as expressing one planet's theme in two different registers.

## Sun sign and full chart

Your sun sign is the sign the Sun occupied when you were born, and it is what most people mean by "my sign". It is only one of many placements in a birth chart. The Moon, the rising sign and each planet also fall in signs, and astrologers weigh them together, so two people with the same sun sign can be described very differently.
