## Friendship in the eyes of traditional BaZi

If you’ve looked at a BaZi chart before, you may have seen a Ten Gods label sometimes translated as “Friend.” That name refers to a relationship between stems within a chart. A BaZi compatibility calculator takes two charts and checks the Day Pillar symbols between them. That comparison uses the same rules here as it does for any pair.

The Day Master is the stem of the Day Pillar and traditionally stands for the person. The Day Branch is the symbol beneath it, traditionally associated with private life. You can find those two symbols in each chart and follow them into the breakdown. Start with an item that reminds you of something about how you spend time together.

## Four kinds of connection between friends

The stem and branch rules have names and images that can give you different ways into a conversation. A few of them are especially easy to illustrate through things friends do together.

A Day Master combination such as Bing (丙) with Xin (辛) pairs stems of opposite yin and yang polarity. If that reminds you of a difference you enjoy, tell your friend about a time it gave you a perspective you wouldn’t have found on your own.

Zi (子) and Chou (丑) form a six-harmony pair. As an image of ease, it might bring to mind the friend you can see for a cup of tea with no plan beyond sitting and talking.

You (酉) and Chou (丑) belong to the same three-harmony metal group. The image of shared direction might remind you of a hobby you’ve kept up together or a cause you both give your time to.

Yin (寅) and Shen (申) form both a clash and a punishment pair, so both lines appear for that pair. If you associate the labels with different ways of doing things, think about a difference you’ve learned to enjoy or accommodate over time.

## Long friendships and changing rhythms

Friendships change shape over the years. Moves, new jobs, partners and children all shift how often people see each other. The idea of different rhythms can prompt a check-in. If you haven’t heard from a friend recently, you could ask how life is going and what would make it easier to catch up. A quick voice message might suit the week better than trying to find a whole evening.

The element complement lines report how much of each person’s first two favorable elements appear in the other’s chart. Their numbers can differ. If that brings support to mind, think of a friend who usually does the checking in. You could thank them for the last call or ask when they’d like you to make the next one.

If you mostly keep in touch by text, the whole comparison needn’t be a sit-down conversation. Choose a memory and send a note about it. Your friend may remember a completely different detail. You can pick up the thread next time you talk, whether that’s tomorrow or months from now.

## Using the result between friends

If both of you are curious, pick a line and tell each other what it brings to mind. It might send you back to something you’ve laughed about for years, or one of you might draw a blank. Both reactions are part of the conversation.

If you’re looking with a small group of friends, ask before entering anyone’s details and let people take part on their own terms. Different pairs can have different lines in the breakdown. Give each pair room to decide what, if anything, they want to discuss.

This comparison uses traditional methods and is offered for reference and self-reflection only. It does not predict how a friendship will develop. The memories you bring to the conversation are yours.
