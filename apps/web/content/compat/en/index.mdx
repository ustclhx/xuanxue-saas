## What the BaZi compatibility calculator compares

A BaZi chart turns a birth date and time into four pillars: year, month, day and hour. Each pillar has a Heavenly Stem on top and an Earthly Branch underneath, which is why the system is also called the Four Pillars of Destiny. A Chinese zodiac animal comes from the year branch. This four-pillars comparison uses the Day Pillar for its direct pairings and the full charts for its element calculations.

The stem of the Day Pillar is the Day Master. In traditional BaZi it represents the person whose chart is being read. The branch beneath it is the Day Branch, a position traditionally associated with a spouse and with private life. These two positions give you a way to locate the symbols you see in the result within each chart.

First the calculator checks the two Day Masters for a stem combination. Then it checks the two Day Branches for four types of pairing. Finally, it uses the weighted shares of the five elements across all available pillars in both charts. The year and month pillars contribute to those shares, as does the hour pillar when you enter a birth time. Every point in the result comes from one of the items in the breakdown.

## Day Master combinations and the twelve branches

Ten Heavenly Stems pair up into five classical combinations. Jia (甲) combines with Ji (己), Yi (乙) with Geng (庚), Bing (丙) with Xin (辛), Ding (丁) with Ren (壬), and Wu (戊) with Gui (癸). Each pair consists of two stems of opposite yin and yang polarity. In the five-element cycle, one stem’s element controls the other’s. Those are descriptions of the chart symbols. When two Day Masters form one of these pairs, the score gains 15 points.

The twelve Earthly Branches have more relationships, and the calculator scores four of them between the two Day Branches.

- **Six-harmony pairing** matches one branch with one partner: Zi (子) and Chou (丑), Yin (寅) and Hai (亥), Mao (卯) and Xu (戌), Chen (辰) and You (酉), Si (巳) and Shen (申), Wu (午) and Wei (未). It adds 12 points.
- **Three-harmony groups** contain branches that sit four places apart: Shen–Zi–Chen, Hai–Mao–Wei, Yin–Wu–Xu and Si–You–Chou. Any two different branches from one set add 8 points.
- **Clash** sets each branch against the one directly opposite: Zi and Wu, Chou and Wei, Yin and Shen, Mao and You, Chen and Xu, Si and Hai. It subtracts 15 points.
- **Punishment** subtracts 8 points for Zi with Mao, any two distinct branches from Yin–Si–Shen or Chou–Xu–Wei, or the same branch on both sides when that branch is Chen, Wu, You, or Hai.

These rules can overlap. Si (巳) and Shen (申) form both a six-harmony pair and a punishment pair, adding 12 and subtracting 8. Yin (寅) and Shen (申) qualify for both clash and punishment, subtracting 15 and 8. Each rule gets its own line in the breakdown. The calculation uses the four branch rules listed above.

## How favorable elements shape the score

Two more scoring items look beyond the Day Pillar. Every chart has weighted shares for wood, fire, earth, metal, and water. The calculator orders the favorable elements according to its chart-strength method, then uses the first two from each person’s list. “Favorable” is a technical term for this calculation.

The calculator finds your two favorable elements, then measures their weighted share in the other person's chart. If those two elements make up 40% or more of the other chart, you get the full 10 points for that direction. Below 40%, the points scale down proportionally and are rounded. The same calculation runs the other way, from their favorable elements into your chart, for another 0 to 10 points.

The directions can differ because they use different element lists and different charts. Suppose the elements from your list make up 36% of the other chart and theirs make up 12% of yours. The lines contribute 9 and 3 points, respectively. If that brings support to mind, use the topic to ask what each of you finds helpful in daily life.

## Reading the number with care

Every comparison starts at 60. The calculator adds the point value for each applicable rule and both element calculations, then limits the total to the 0–100 range. It displays a reference score without grades or tiers. The inputs are the charts and the fixed scoring rules. Kindness, trust, and how you handle a disagreement are things you learn from experience with each other.

Open the breakdown and pick one line that brings a real experience to mind. A Day Master combination might prompt you to talk about what first got you talking. A clash could be a way into a discussion about schedules or how each of you recharges. If you start with element complement, ask what each of you appreciates and what you would like more of. You may both remember the same moment differently.

The comparison is calculated with traditional methods and is offered for reference and self-reflection only. It does not predict the future. Decisions about a relationship belong to the people in it.

## What to enter and what happens to the data

Enter each person’s birth date and birthplace or time zone. Add their birth time if you know it. Mark an unknown time on the form and the calculator uses three pillars. This tool changes the Day Pillar at 11 p.m. in the adjusted local time used for the chart, so a known time around the day boundary can affect the Day Pillar checks as well as the element shares. The birth time can also matter near the solar terms used to set the year and month pillars.

Please ask before entering someone else's birth details. The free calculator uses the other person's data only to produce this result and does not store it by default. A new result link includes the scenario, the score, and the point breakdown. It leaves out both people’s birth details. If a detailed reading is available and you purchase it, both sets of birth details are saved with the report for calculation and later access. Deleting the report also deletes those details.
