## What BaZi compatibility actually compares

A BaZi chart turns a birth date and time into four pillars: year, month, day and hour. Each pillar has a Heavenly Stem on top and an Earthly Branch underneath, which is why the system is also called the Four Pillars of Destiny. When people search for a four pillars compatibility check, they often expect a comparison of zodiac animals. That is the year branch, and it is the most public, generational layer of the chart. Classical BaZi practice puts much more weight on the Day Pillar.

The stem of the Day Pillar is the Day Master. It stands for the person themselves: temperament, the way they meet the world, what kind of support they draw on. The branch underneath it, the Day Branch, sits in the position traditionally associated with a spouse or the closest companion, and more broadly with private, everyday life. Comparing two Day Pillars is therefore the most direct way traditional methods relate one person to another.

This calculator keeps to that core. It compares the two Day Masters, the two Day Branches, and then the balance of the five elements across both charts. It does not look at year or month pillars, symbolic stars or luck pillars. The hour pillar matters only because it changes the element proportions of a chart. Keeping the method narrow makes every point in the result traceable to one named rule.

## Day Master combinations and the twelve branches

Ten Heavenly Stems pair up into five classical combinations. Jia (甲) combines with Ji (己), Yi (乙) with Geng (庚), Bing (丙) with Xin (辛), Ding (丁) with Ren (壬), and Wu (戊) with Gui (癸). In each pair, one stem controls the other in the five-element cycle, and the two are also yin and yang opposites. Old texts describe this as attraction between unlike partners, a little like a lock and key. When two Day Masters form one of these pairs, the score gains 15 points.

The twelve Earthly Branches have more relationships, and the calculator scores four of them between the two Day Branches.

- **Six harmony** pairs one branch with one partner: Zi (子) and Chou (丑), Yin (寅) and Hai (亥), Mao (卯) and Xu (戌), Chen (辰) and You (酉), Si (巳) and Shen (申), Wu (午) and Wei (未). It adds 12 points.
- **Three harmony** groups branches that sit four places apart: Shen–Zi–Chen, Hai–Mao–Wei, Yin–Wu–Xu and Si–You–Chou. Any two different branches from one set add 8 points.
- **Clash** sets each branch against the one directly opposite: Zi and Wu, Chou and Wei, Yin and Shen, Mao and You, Chen and Xu, Si and Hai. It subtracts 15 points.
- **Punishment** covers Zi with Mao; the Yin, Si and Shen trio; the Chou, Xu and Wei trio; and self-punishment when both people have Chen, Wu, You or Hai. It subtracts 8 points.

These can overlap. Si (巳) and Shen (申) are a six harmony and a punishment at once, so both items appear. Yin (寅) and Shen (申) are a clash and a punishment. The calculator lists each hit separately so you can see exactly why the number moved. The traditional "harm" relationship is not scored.

## How favorable elements shape the score

The second half of the comparison looks beyond the Day Pillar. Every chart has a mix of wood, fire, earth, metal and water, and a practitioner usually names one or two favorable elements that would bring the chart into better balance. A chart heavy in water and metal, for example, might favor wood and fire.

The calculator finds your two favorable elements, then measures their weighted share in the other person's chart. If those two elements make up 40% or more of the other chart, you get the full 10 points for that direction. Below 40%, the points scale down proportionally and are rounded. The same calculation runs the other way, from their favorable elements into your chart, for another 0 to 10 points.

Reading the two directions side by side is often more interesting than the total. A 9 and a 3 tell a different story from two sixes. In traditional language, one chart supplies what the other lacks more than the reverse. In everyday terms, it can prompt a question about where each of you feels supported and where you feel you are doing the supporting.

## Reading the number with care

Every comparison starts at 60, which is neither a pass nor a fail. Points are added or taken away, and the result is clamped between 0 and 100. There are no grades, labels or tiers, because the number only counts how many traditional rules happen to match between two dates. It does not measure kindness, trust, shared values or the work two people put in, and those are what most relationships rest on.

A practical way to use the result is to open the breakdown and treat each line as a conversation prompt. A Day Master combination might lead you to talk about what drew you together. A clash between Day Branches can open a discussion about schedules, energy levels and how each of you recharges. A lopsided element complement can lead to a talk about give and take. Read this way, the score becomes a structured way to think about two temperaments rather than a judgment on the relationship.

The comparison is calculated with traditional methods and is offered for reference and self-reflection only. It does not predict the future, and it should never be the reason for a major personal decision.

## What to enter and what happens to the data

You need each person's date of birth, and ideally a birth time. If you are unsure of a time, mark it as unknown and that chart will use three pillars. The Day Pillar is set by the date alone, so the combination, harmony, clash and punishment items stay exactly the same, and only the element shares shift.

Please ask before entering someone else's birth details. The free calculator uses the other person's data only to produce this result and does not store it by default. If you share a result, the link carries the score and the names of the items that applied, never either birth date. If you choose to buy a detailed reading, both sets of birth details are saved with that report so it can be generated and reopened, and deleting the report deletes them too.
