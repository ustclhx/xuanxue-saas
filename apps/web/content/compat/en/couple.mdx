## A BaZi compatibility check when you’re dating

When you start seeing someone, there are lots of small things to learn. You might know their favorite restaurant and still have no idea how they like to spend a Sunday. If you’re curious about a BaZi compatibility calculator, start by looking at the two charts together and choosing one thing you’d both like to talk about.

A BaZi chart uses the year, month, day, and hour pillars when the time is known. The stem in the Day Pillar is the Day Master, traditionally used to represent the person. The branch below it is associated with private life and close partnership. The calculator compares those two positions between charts, then looks at the five elements across each chart. The examples on this page focus on dating.

## Attraction, ease and pace in the early months

A Day Master combination, such as Jia (甲) with Ji (己) or Wu (戊) with Gui (癸), pairs stems with opposite yin and yang polarity. You can use the image of two unlike things coming together to compare what you remember about your first date. You may each have noticed something the other person completely missed.

Six-harmony and three-harmony pairings between Day Branches offer images of ease and shared direction. Think of a time you sat together without needing to fill the silence, or a plan you both got excited about. Ask what the other person remembers about it.

Clash and punishment are two distinct rules. Zi (子) and Wu (午) sit opposite each other on the branch circle and form a clash. The contrast can be a way to bring up how you make plans. One person might like knowing what the weekend holds on Wednesday, while the other enjoys deciding on the day. Ask what each of you likes about their own way of planning.

## Talking through the breakdown together

The most useful way to read the result is side by side. Open the breakdown and take one line at a time. For each item, ask whether the traditional description fits your experience so far. Sometimes it will, sometimes it will not, and both outcomes are interesting.

The two element complement lines are a good place to slow down. They show how much of each person's favorable elements appear in the other's chart. Those numbers describe the charts. If the topic of support comes to mind, try a question about something you’ve done together already. You could ask what felt reassuring the last time plans changed, or what each of you enjoyed about taking the lead.

If a clash or punishment line makes you think of a habit, each of you could name one thing you appreciate and one thing you’d like to understand better. Take turns listening to the answer.

Ask your partner before entering their birth details. If they’re interested, look at the result together and let either person decide when they’ve seen enough. A question you both enjoy answering can make for a better conversation than working through every line.

You can also trade examples from different stages of dating. Maybe you remember the first plan you made together, while your partner remembers the first time you changed a plan and both enjoyed where it led. Give each other time to finish the story. The details you each remember may open up the next question.

## Keeping the number in proportion

The score starts at 60. The tool applies the stem and branch rules, adds the two favorable-element contributions, and keeps the result in the 0–100 range. The lines in the breakdown account for the number. The way you handle the next disagreement is up to both of you.

This comparison uses traditional methods and is offered for reference and self-reflection only. It does not predict the future of your relationship.
