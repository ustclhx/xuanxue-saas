## Why daters look up four pillars compatibility

When people start seeing someone, curiosity runs high. Searching for a BaZi compatibility calculator is usually less about getting an answer and more about having a new lens: what does an old system say about the two of us, and does any of it ring true? That is a reasonable way to approach it.

A BaZi chart is built from four pillars of birth data, and the most personal of them is the Day Pillar. Its stem, the Day Master, represents you, and its branch reflects the private side of life, including close partnership. Comparing two Day Pillars is the traditional way to set one person beside another. This page applies that comparison to dating couples and explains each result in terms that fit the early and middle stages of a relationship.

## Attraction, ease and pace in the early months

Classical texts give three broad signals between two Day Pillars. The first is attraction, carried by the five Day Master combinations, such as Jia (甲) with Ji (己) or Wu (戊) with Gui (癸). These pairs are opposites in yin and yang that complement each other, which is why the old books compare them to two halves fitting together.

The second is ease, carried by six harmony and three harmony between the Day Branches. A harmony suggests the kind of comfort that appears after the excitement of the first dates, when you can sit in the same room without needing to fill the silence.

The third is pace, which is where clash and punishment come in. These do not describe conflict so much as different speeds. Someone with a Zi (子) Day Branch and someone with a Wu (午) Day Branch sit opposite each other on the branch circle. In a dating context, that might be one person who likes to plan a week ahead and another who decides on the day. Knowing this in advance can turn a potential irritation into a running joke you both understand.

## Talking through the breakdown together

The most useful way to read the result is side by side. Open the breakdown and take one line at a time. For each item, ask whether the traditional description fits your experience so far. Sometimes it will, sometimes it will not, and both outcomes are interesting.

The two element complement lines are a good place to slow down. They show how much of each person's favorable elements appear in the other's chart. If one direction is much higher, you might talk about who tends to lead plans, who offers reassurance, and whether that balance feels right to both of you. These are conversations many couples have eventually anyway. The chart just gives you a neutral way to begin.

If the result shows a clash or punishment, try a simple exercise: each of you names one habit of the other that you appreciate and one you would like to understand better. Keep it light.

One caution: please ask before entering your partner's birth details, and avoid springing the result on them as evidence in an argument. A chart comparison works well as a shared curiosity and badly as a debating point. If one of you is more interested in BaZi than the other, that is fine too. Let the less curious partner set how deep the conversation goes.

## Keeping the number in proportion

The score starts at 60 and moves up or down according to fixed traditional rules. It cannot see how you treat each other, how you handle disagreements, or what you want from the relationship. A couple with a modest score can be very happy, and a high score does not make anything easy.

The comparison uses traditional methods and is meant for reference and self-reflection; it does not predict the future of your relationship. Use it as one more way to get to know each other, and let your own experience together carry more weight than any number.
