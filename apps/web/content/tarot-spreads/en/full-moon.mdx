## Why the full moon

The full moon comes roughly two weeks after a new moon, about halfway through the cycle of phases. From Earth, the Moon appears roughly opposite the Sun, and almost all of the face we see is sunlit. A complete cycle, from one new moon to the next, averages about 29.5 days. This spread uses that rhythm as a reminder to look back at the past few weeks.

The phase of the Moon has no effect on the cards. The box above shows the phase for your local date and the next full moon date when lunar data is available. The exact moment of the full moon can fall during daylight where you live. To follow the cycle night by night, see [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today), or use the [full moon calendar](/full-moon-calendar) to check the date and time of the exact phase.

## How the four positions fit together

The spread moves in one direction: from what you noticed, through what you release and what you keep, to a question you take forward. Reading the cards in that order turns the full moon from a single night into a short review of the weeks behind you.

The second and third positions are a pair. Lay them side by side and ask whether they are talking about the same area of your life. If the card to set down is the Four of Pentacles (security, conservation) and the card to keep is the Queen of Pentacles (resourcefulness, care), the spread may be separating two ways of handling the same resources: holding on out of fear, and looking after things out of care.

Start with the question raised by the first card. When it is The Moon itself, whose keywords are uncertainty and imagination, you might recall something you sensed without being sure. The Ace of Swords (clarity, insight) in the same place can prompt you to name something you understand better now. Read the other cards with that example in mind. For the full entry on any card, see the [tarot meanings](/tarot/cards) library.

## Keeping a full moon record

Because full moons come around about once a month, you can make this spread a regular entry in a journal. Write down the date, the four cards and one sentence for each position. After a few months, read the notes in order. If a question keeps appearing in slightly different words, underline it and see whether your answers have changed since the first entry. You can compare what you noticed about a situation at the time with what you remember about it now.

A record also makes the last position more useful. Next month, before you shuffle, reread the question you wrote down and note what happened with it. Then let the new first card describe what has come to light since. Keep the notes short. A line per card is enough, and a record you actually keep is worth more than a detailed one you abandon after the second month.

If you want to compare entries, jot down one observation from the weeks in between. A habit you found easier to keep or a plan you changed gives you something concrete to revisit.

## Drawing outside the full moon

Choose an evening when you have time to reflect. For a day or two before or after the full phase, the Moon can still look nearly round and serve as a visual reminder.

You can pair it with a one-card draw at the new moon, about two weeks later. Use the full moon to review what happened and the new moon to consider what you want to give your attention to next. The same four positions can mark the end of a project, a season or a calendar month. If you draw the Ten of Swords, with its keywords ending and exhaustion, in the second position, ask whether there is something you already feel ready to let go of.
