## Why the full moon

The full moon falls roughly halfway through the cycle of phases, about two weeks after a new moon, when the Moon sits opposite the Sun in the sky and its whole visible face is lit. A complete cycle, from one new moon to the next, averages about 29.5 days. That steady rhythm makes the full moon a natural checkpoint, and in modern tarot practice many readers use it as a time to look back before starting again.

That is a custom for reflection. The phase of the Moon has no effect on the cards, and nothing in this spread depends on the exact hour of the full moon. ## How the four positions fit together

The spread moves in one direction: from what you noticed, through what you release and what you keep, to a question you take forward. Reading the cards in that order turns the full moon from a single night into a short review of the weeks behind you.

The second and third positions are a pair. Lay them side by side and ask whether they are talking about the same area of your life. If the card to set down is the Four of Pentacles (security, conservation) and the card to keep is the Queen of Pentacles (resourcefulness, care), the spread may be separating two ways of handling the same resources: holding on out of fear, and looking after things out of care.

The first card often explains the other three. When it is The Moon itself, whose keywords are uncertainty and imagination, the review may be about something you sensed without being sure. The Ace of Swords (clarity, insight) in the same place suggests something has become plain enough to act on. Either way, the remaining cards are best read as a response to that first discovery. ## Keeping a full moon record

Because full moons come round about once a month, this spread works well as a series. Write down the date, the four cards and one sentence for each position. After three or four months, read the notes in order. Patterns show up that no single spread could reveal: a card that keeps appearing in the second position, or a question for the next cycle that you keep asking in slightly different words.

A record also makes the last position more useful. Next month, before you shuffle, reread the question you wrote down and note what happened with it. Then let the new first card describe what has come to light since. Keep the notes short. A line per card is enough, and a record you actually keep is worth more than a detailed one you abandon after the second month.

## Drawing outside the full moon

Nothing in the spread requires the exact night. If the date falls on a busy night, a day or two either side works just as well, since the Moon still looks almost round to the eye.

Some readers pair it with a lighter draw at the new moon, about two weeks later, keeping the review for the full moon and a single forward-looking card for the new one. Others use the same four positions at the end of a project, a season or a calendar month, where the idea of closing a cycle fits just as well. When a heavy card appears, such as the Ten of Swords with its keywords ending and exhaustion, read it in the second position as something that has already run its course rather than as a warning.
