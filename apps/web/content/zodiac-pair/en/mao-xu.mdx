## How the two branches relate

Underneath the harmony runs a controlling line: Mao Wood controls Xu Earth, the way spring roots split dry ground. The pair therefore joins in one sense and checks in another, a combination worth holding in mind. In the groups, Mao is the center of the Hai–Mao–Wei Wood triangle, and Xu is the storage stage of the Yin–Wu–Xu Fire triangle. That placement explains the Fire transformation neatly: the strongest Wood meets the vault where Fire is kept. To see Xu beside the opening member of its own Fire group, compare this page with [Tiger and Dog compatibility](/chinese-zodiac-compatibility/tiger-and-dog).

## Pace and rhythm together

Between 05:00 and 07:00, the Mao hour, light arrives and the day is still unhurried. Between 19:00 and 21:00, the Xu hour, the light is gone and the day's work is being put away. Twelve hours separate the two, almost exactly a half turn of the clock, and yet the harmony links them like the two ends of a bridge.

Read as a rhythm, the Rabbit side tends to begin gently, preparing and arranging before anyone else asks for anything. The Dog side tends to finish faithfully, checking that the doors are locked and every promise has been met. The spring equinox is a time of planting; late autumn is the last harvest and the storing of grain. Between them lies the whole growing season.

For friends, family members, coworkers or partners, this pattern suggests that the most natural division is by bookends: one person shapes the morning, the other closes the evening. The middle of the day is shared ground, where both need to say what they are carrying. A short message at noon, even one line, keeps the two halves connected. ## What each can learn from the other

Dawn and dusk mirror each other, and the harmony suggests each holds a lesson the other could use. Choose two or three of these questions to think about on your own:

- When do I prepare well but then leave the closing to someone else?
- Do I finish things so carefully that I rarely start new ones?
- Which of my evening routines exists only out of habit?
- What would I plant if I knew someone would bring the harvest in?
- When did someone's steadiness protect a plan of mine?

A small exercise follows from the image. For two weeks, the person who opens the day writes one line each morning about what they hope gets done. The person who closes the day writes one line each evening about what actually did. Read both columns together at the end, and note where hopes and results met and where they drifted apart.

## Where friction tends to show up

A six harmony describes attraction; it says nothing about how two people handle a busy Tuesday. The twelve-hour gap is the clearest source of difference. One person is fresh when the other is tired, and conversations that matter can land at the wrong end of someone's day.

There is also the element line. Wood controlling Earth can show up as one person rearranging the other's carefully kept order in the name of a fresh start. Suggestions that help:

1. Pick one regular time, perhaps a weekend morning, for decisions that need both people fully awake.
2. Agree that the person who keeps the routine is consulted before it changes.
3. When one of you starts something new, ask the other what it would take to finish it, before beginning.

Keeping these as standing agreements, rather than renegotiating each time, saves energy on both sides.

## Reading two full BaZi charts

For an illustration, take 1975 (Yi Mao) and 1970 (Geng Xu). The year branches form the Mao–Xu six harmony. The stems form their own pair: Yi is yin Wood and Geng is yang Metal, which classical texts list as a stem combination, while in the controlling cycle Metal checks Wood.

Remember that the year animal is a single branch, sitting among eight characters spread over four pillars. A You in either chart would clash with Mao, and a Chen would clash with Xu. A Yin or Wu in the 1975 chart would join Xu's Fire group, and a Hai or Wei in the 1970 chart would join Mao's Wood group. Cast both charts from birth date and birth hour, and look at every pillar before drawing any conclusion from the year alone.
