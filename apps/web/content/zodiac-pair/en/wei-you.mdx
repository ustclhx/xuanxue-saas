## How the two branches relate

Wei Earth generates You Metal, so the support runs from the Goat toward the Rooster. Dry earth is a less obvious source of metal than damp earth, which classical texts note: it holds heat and stored Wood rather than moisture. You, the receiving branch, is the peak of the Si–You–Chou Metal group, Metal at its most refined. Wei closes the Hai–Mao–Wei Wood group as its storehouse. So the giving branch carries an element, Wood, that the receiving branch traditionally cuts. Support and a faint counterweight sit in the same pair. To see the Goat beside the peak of its own group, [Rabbit and Goat compatibility](/chinese-zodiac-compatibility/rabbit-and-goat) reads that combination.

## Pace and rhythm together

Wei's hour spans 13:00 to 15:00, the warm, slow early afternoon. You's hour spans 17:00 to 19:00, when work ends and the light goes golden and then gray. Between them, the Shen hour fills the busiest stretch of the afternoon. One branch is the drowsy middle of the day, the other its orderly close.

Friends, siblings, colleagues or partners with these branches might find a natural sequence here. The Goat image leans toward comfort, care and accommodating others, keeping a space livable. The Rooster image leans toward order, precision and finishing touches, keeping it in good shape. The Goat side may tend the process; the Rooster side may tend the result.

The handover from Wei to You is a handover from warmth to clarity. Late summer still allows long, easy days; mid-autumn asks for counting and sorting before winter. In shared work, let one person carry the comfortable middle stretch, where patience matters, and the other close each phase with a review. Agree when that closing review happens. Otherwise the Goat side may feel inspected without warning, and the Rooster side may feel that nothing ever gets wrapped up.

## What each can learn from the other

Earth holds and accommodates; Metal defines and separates. Each element does something the other finds hard. Questions to ask yourself:

- When I accommodate others, do I also name what I need?
- When I set a standard, do I explain why it matters to me?
- Which of my comforts have quietly become clutter?
- Where would a little more warmth make my precision easier to receive?

An exercise: choose one shared space, a desk, a kitchen shelf, a calendar. For one week the Goat side arranges it purely for comfort. The following week the Rooster side arranges it purely for order. At the end, keep one element from each version and write down what you chose.

## Where friction tends to show up

Treat the shared yin imagery as a prompt to check for unspoken expectations. For example, has everyone agreed on the standard before work begins? The Goat side may feel that care goes unacknowledged or is measured against a standard it did not agree to. The Rooster side may feel that its attention to detail is seen as fussiness rather than contribution.

Concrete arrangements turn these into adjustments. State standards before a task starts, not after it is done. Name care when you notice it, specifically: what was done, and how it helped. Divide tasks so that each person owns some areas fully, rather than one person always finishing what the other started. When feedback is needed, ask whether the other person wants it now or later. ## Reading two full BaZi charts

Suppose one person was born in 2003 (Gui Wei) and the other in 2005 (Yi You). Gui is yin Water and Yi is yin Wood, and in the stem cycle Water generates Wood, so the stems echo the generating flow of the branches, Earth to Metal, with Water to Wood above. Inside each pillar, though, the lines run differently: Water on Earth in 2003, which Earth controls, and Wood on Metal in 2005, which Metal controls.

The animal is just the year branch, one of eight characters. A Chou in either chart would clash with Wei, a Mao would clash with You, a Wu would form a six harmony with Wei, and a Chen would form one with You. Those other pillars add context that a pairing of the year branches cannot show. Cast both charts from complete birth data, including the hour, and compare the four pillars of each before reading anything into the year branches alone.
