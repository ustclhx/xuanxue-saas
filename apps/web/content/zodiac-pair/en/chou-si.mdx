## How the two branches relate

The element arrow points from the Snake to the Ox: Si Fire generates Chou Earth. Warmth from the forge settles into the ground, and the ground in turn becomes the place where finished Metal rests. That makes the pair doubly linked, once by group membership and once by a generating direction, and both run the same way, from Si toward Chou. Si holds the opening corner of the Metal triangle and Chou the closing one. The middle corner belongs to You, so the Rooster is the missing piece whenever these two are read together. For the Snake's own branch set against the coming year pillar, see [Snake in 2027](/chinese-zodiac-2027/snake).

## Pace and rhythm together

Late morning, 09:00 to 11:00, is the Si hour: the day's business is underway and the heat is rising. The Chou hour runs from 01:00 to 03:00, the stillest part of the night. These two hours sit almost opposite on the clock, yet the Metal story binds them, and that combination gives an interesting tempo.

Picture the Snake side as the one who gets things moving in public, choosing materials, setting the first shape, warming the room. Picture the Ox side as the one who, much later and often unseen, makes sure what was made gets finished, labeled and stored where it can be found. Early summer, from Start of Summer to Grain in Ear, is when crops set their grain; late winter, from Minor Cold to Li Chun, is when tools are repaired and seed is checked. One season produces, the other preserves.

For any two people, whether friends running an event, coworkers on a release or relatives managing a family home, the handover tends to come late and quietly. Agree on a moment when the Si-side work counts as done and the Chou-side keeping begins, and mark that moment out loud.

## What each can learn from the other

The forge and the storeroom need each other, but each also has a habit worth borrowing. A few self-questions, one set for each image:

From the Snake's side of the triangle:
- Do I finish shaping something and then lose interest before it is put to use?
- Who keeps track of what I made, and do I know where they put it?

From the Ox's side:
- Am I storing things that would serve better if they were brought back into the light?
- When did I last start something new rather than maintain something old?

One exercise works well for this pair: choose an old project, perhaps a box of photographs or an unfinished document, and take it through the whole Metal cycle together. One person reheats it, the other files the result. Talk afterward about which half felt like work and which felt like rest. ## Where friction tends to show up

The hours are far apart, and that distance is where most misunderstandings start. One person is busiest before noon; the other does their deepest work when everyone else is asleep. Messages arrive at inconvenient moments, and finished work may sit unacknowledged for a day.

Tempo differs too. Si energy is quick to commit to a shape; Chou energy prefers to wait until the shape has proved itself. Neither is a flaw, but each can look like one from the other side. Try these:

1. Write down when each of you is reachable, and treat those windows as the default for anything that needs a reply.
2. Let the starter state what is still open before handing over, so the keeper does not inherit hidden loose ends.
3. Before storing or archiving anything shared, ask once whether the other person still wants it in use.

When tension rises, describe the stage the work is in, shaping or keeping, before describing how you feel about it.

## Reading two full BaZi charts

Picture someone born in 1965 (Yi Si) beside someone born in 1961 (Xin Chou). Their year branches belong to the Metal group. The stems add a different direction: Xin is Metal and Yi is Wood, and Metal controls Wood, so the year pillars contain a checking relation alongside the group bond.

Consider the Snake and the Ox as two letters in eight-letter words. A month or day branch of You in either chart would complete the Metal triangle. A Hai branch would clash with Si, and a Wei branch would clash with Chou. Si also forms a six harmony with Shen, and Chou forms one with Zi. Any of these can sit in the month, day or hour pillar and change the tone of the whole comparison. Cast both charts from date and time of birth, then read them side by side, pillar after pillar.
