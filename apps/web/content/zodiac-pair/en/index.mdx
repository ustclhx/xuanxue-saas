## How to read the compatibility chart

Find your own animal in the row headings, then move across to the column of the other person's animal. The square where they meet shows one of four marks: three harmony, six harmony, clash or harm. The Dragon row and the Rooster column, for example, meet at a six harmony square, because Chen and You are one of the six harmony pairs. Swap the row and the column and you land on a square with the same mark, linking to the same page.

Shading carries meaning too. Filled squares are harmonies, either three harmony or six harmony. Outlined squares are tensions, either a clash or a harm. Squares on the diagonal read "Same", for two people born under the same animal. A dash means none of the four marked relations applies, and it does not mean the two branches have nothing to say to each other. The Goat and the Dog, for instance, show a dash, yet Wei and Xu are classed as both a punishment and a break. Those two relations are left off the chart to keep it readable, and every pair page lists them in full.

Some pairs collect several relations at once. The Snake and the Monkey (Si and Shen) are a six harmony, a punishment and a break all together. When that happens, the chart shows the first that applies in this order: six harmony, three harmony, clash, harm. On a phone, the grid becomes two pickers and a list of all twelve pairings for the first animal you choose.

## Where harmony and clash come from

Traditional Chinese astrology placed the twelve branches around a compass. Set them in a circle and Zi lands due north, Wu due south, Mao due east and You due west. A clash is simply a pair of branches facing each other across that circle. There are six: Zi–Wu, Chou–Wei, Yin–Shen, Mao–You, Chen–Xu and Si–Hai.

Three harmony groups follow the life cycle of an element through birth, peak and storage. Shen, Zi and Chen form the Water group; Si, You and Chou the Metal group; Yin, Wu and Xu the Fire group; Hai, Mao and Wei the Wood group. A three harmony square means the two branches belong to the same group. Older texts treat a complete group as needing all three members, so a pair such as Shen and Chen is half of the Water group, waiting for Zi in the middle.

The six harmony pairs are Zi–Chou, Yin–Hai, Mao–Xu, Chen–You, Si–Shen and Wu–Wei. Harm follows from the harmonies and clashes together: Zi pairs with Chou, Wei clashes with Chou, and so Zi and Wei are counted as a harm. For each branch's element, direction and the two ways of dating the zodiac year, the overview of [Chinese zodiac years](/chinese-zodiac) collects the basics in one place.

None of these rules was designed to rank one pairing above another. They describe positions on a circle and the seasons those positions stand for. The pair pages read them that way: as images of two rhythms, with practical notes on where those rhythms meet and where they pull apart.

## Reading two full BaZi charts

Your zodiac animal is the branch of your year pillar. A BaZi chart has four pillars, year, month, day and hour, each with a heavenly stem and an earthly branch, so the animal is one of eight characters. The birth years on every pair page start from that year's Li Chun, so if your birthday is in January or early February, check the note under the year table first.

Year branches alone put the Rabbit and the Rooster in a clash, Mao against You. If the Rabbit's day branch is Chen, though, it forms a six harmony with the Rooster's You, and the month branches may add another link or another tension. That is why a traditional comparison of two charts looks at the day pillars, the month branches and the balance of elements across all eight characters.
