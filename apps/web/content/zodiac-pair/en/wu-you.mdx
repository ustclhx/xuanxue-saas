## How the two branches relate

Wu Fire controls You Metal; the arrow runs from south to west. Classical commentators often read this as tempering: heat applied to metal so that it can be worked. In the group structure, Wu is the peak of the Yin–Wu–Xu Fire group and You the peak of the Si–You–Chou Metal group. Each is the strongest expression of its element. The Rooster's group closes with the Ox as storage; for that pairing, see [Ox and Rooster](/chinese-zodiac-compatibility/ox-and-rooster).

## Pace and rhythm together

At noon, the Wu hour, the sun is overhead and shadows almost disappear. Everything is visible. By dusk, the You hour, light comes in long and low, and the edges of things stand out sharply. Two people borrowing these images might find that one tends to act in full view, openly and quickly, while the other prefers to judge carefully once the glare has passed.

The seasonal gap is wider. Wu is midsummer, around the summer solstice, when daylight is longest in the Northern Hemisphere. You is mid-autumn, around the equinox, when the light has balanced and harvest is weighed. A quarter of the year lies between them, just as a quarter of the day does.

Whether the two are friends, colleagues, relatives or partners, the afternoon between them is shared working time. The Horse side may want decisions at midday; the Rooster side may want them at the end of the day, after reflection. Splitting the difference works: raise the question at noon, close it at dusk. That rhythm gives the Fire side the visibility it values and the Metal side the time it needs. The handover point is mid-afternoon, a good moment for a short check-in.

## What each can learn from the other

Full sun shows everything at once; low sun shows the outlines. The Horse image suggests momentum and openness; the Rooster image, discernment and finish. Questions to borrow:

- When I act quickly, do I leave a moment for someone else to sharpen the plan?
- When I weigh carefully, am I aware of the cost of waiting?
- What did the other person see that I missed because I was looking at a different hour?

An exercise: take one decision the two of you face. Write the Horse version, made in a single sitting at noon. Write the Rooster version, made after a day of reflection. Compare them that evening and pick elements from each. Over a few rounds you may notice which kinds of choices suit the noon approach and which suit the dusk approach, and that map is more useful than either habit alone.

## Where friction tends to show up

Fire controlling Metal can play out as pressure: the faster side pushes, the more exacting side bristles. Since tradition names no clash, harm or break here, the friction is about pace and standards rather than any structural opposition.

Practical arrangements:

1. Set a timeline for decisions that includes at least one pause for review.
2. Agree on what counts as done before the work starts, so the review is not a moving target.
3. When the Rooster side gives detailed feedback, the Horse side can ask which point matters most.
4. When the Horse side wants to move on, the Rooster side can name one thing that must be checked first.

## Reading two full BaZi charts

Take one person born in 2002 (Ren Wu) and another in 2005 (Yi You). Their year branches carry Fire controlling Metal. The stems tell a gentler story: Ren is yang Water, Yi is yin Wood, and Water generates Wood. The two year pillars therefore show a controlling relation between the branches and a generating relation between the stems.

Every chart has eight characters, and the animal is one. A Wei in either chart's month pillar would form the Wu–Wei six harmony with the Horse. A Chen would form the Chen–You six harmony with the Rooster. A Zi or a Mao elsewhere would introduce the Zi–Wu clash or the Mao–You clash. Build both charts from exact birth date and time, then read across the pillars one at a time before assigning meaning to the year branches.
