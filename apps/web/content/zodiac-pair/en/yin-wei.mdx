## How the two branches relate

Yin Wood controls Wei Earth: roots break up dry soil and draw from it. The arrow runs from Tiger to Goat, and it describes how the two elements act on each other rather than which person leads. Group membership points in separate directions. Yin opens the Yin–Wu–Xu Fire group as the birth stage of Fire, since spring Wood is fuel for summer heat. Wei closes the Hai–Mao–Wei Wood group as its storage stage. So the Tiger is Wood that starts a Fire cycle, while the Goat is Earth that ends a Wood cycle, and a reader sees a quiet echo between them. For the Tiger's own branch beside 2027, see [Tiger in 2027](/chinese-zodiac-2027/tiger).

## Pace and rhythm together

Before first light, from 03:00 to 05:00, the Yin hour belongs to people who rise early or never quite went to sleep. The Wei hour, 13:00 to 15:00, is the slow stretch after lunch when heat sits heavily and work becomes routine. The two watches start ten hours apart, with eight hours between the end of Yin and the start of Wei.

This suggests one partner, friend or colleague who launches things in the dark, often alone and with sudden energy, and another who does steady, patient tending in the warm middle of the day. In the season picture, Yin is the first push of spring, when buds open on bare branches; Wei is the ripening stretch of late summer, when what was planted fills out. The Tiger image starts; the Goat image brings to fruit.

The handover works best when it is planned. If the early riser leaves a short written outline, the afternoon worker can pick it up without waiting for a conversation. In a household, the Tiger-side person might handle tasks needing a decisive start, such as booking, organizing or calling, while the Goat-side person handles the long, careful follow-through, such as cooking, mending or checking in on relatives. Discuss which tasks suit an early burst and which suit an afternoon pace.

## What each can learn from the other

The sapling and the summer soil each hold something the other lacks. Use the image to ask a few honest questions:

- When I begin something with energy, do I stay long enough to see it ripen?
- Do I mistake caution for wisdom when it is only comfort?
- What would change if I let another person set the first step?
- Which of my plans has outgrown the ground I prepared for it?

One exercise for two: pick a small garden, real or figurative, such as a shared savings goal or a recurring family dinner. The Yin-side person sets it up in a single burst. The Wei-side person tends it each week for a month without changing the setup. At the end, review together what the setup made easy and what the weekly tending revealed. To see Wei beside the first member of its own Wood group, read [Goat and Pig](/chinese-zodiac-compatibility/goat-and-pig).

## Where friction tends to show up

Wood controlling Earth can show up as one person feeling pushed or rearranged by the other's plans. A Tiger-side habit of acting first and explaining later may feel abrupt; a Goat-side wish to consider everyone's comfort may feel slow. These are differences of tempo, not of goodwill.

Practical arrangements:

1. Before a big change, give a twenty-four-hour notice so the slower-paced person has time to adjust.
2. Divide decisions by type: the early-burst person handles starts and deadlines, the careful person handles upkeep and people.
3. When one feels steamrolled, say so in a single sentence and name the specific decision.
4. Schedule check-ins at a time that suits both clocks, perhaps late morning, between the two hours.

These two can also simply be asleep or busy when the other is most alert. Keeping expectations about reply times explicit removes a lot of quiet resentment.

## Reading two full BaZi charts

Here is an example: 1974 (Jia Yin) for one person and 1979 (Ji Wei) for the other. The branches form no named relation. The stems are a different story: Jia is yang Wood and Ji is yin Earth, and classical texts pair Jia with Ji as a stem combination, while in the controlling cycle Wood still controls Earth.

Every chart holds four pillars and so eight characters, and the year animal speaks for only one. If the Ji Wei chart has a Hai branch, it forms a six harmony with the other person's Yin. If the Jia Yin chart has a Wu branch, it forms a six harmony with Wei. A Shen in either chart would clash with Yin, and a Chou would clash with Wei. Cast both charts from the date and the hour of birth, then compare all four pillars, branch against branch and stem against stem.
