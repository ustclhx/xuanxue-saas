## The 12 Chinese zodiac animals

The animals follow the fixed order of the twelve Earthly Branches: Rat for Zi (子), Ox for Chou (丑), Tiger for Yin (寅), Rabbit for Mao (卯), Dragon for Chen (辰), Snake for Si (巳), Horse for Wu (午), Goat for Wei (未), Monkey for Shen (申), Rooster for You (酉), Dog for Xu (戌) and Pig for Hai (亥). The Wei animal is also translated as Sheep or Ram; this site uses Goat throughout.

Each branch has a fixed element and is either yin or yang. Zi is yang water and Hai is yin water. Yin is yang wood and Mao is yin wood. Wu is yang fire and Si is yin fire. Shen is yang metal and You is yin metal. Four earth branches mark the ends of the seasons: Chen and Xu are yang earth, while Chou and Wei are yin earth. The Dog and the Goat therefore both represent earth branches. The element in a name such as Fire Goat comes from the Heavenly Stem paired with the branch in that particular year.

The branches also map onto the seasons and the compass. Yin, Mao and Chen belong to spring and the east; Si, Wu and Wei to summer and the south; Shen, You and Xu to autumn and the west; Hai, Zi and Chou to winter and the north. When you see an animal described as a winter or summer sign, this is the older picture behind it.

Years move through the branches one step at a time, so the same animal returns every twelve years. ## Li Chun or Chinese New Year: when the animal changes

There are two common answers to the question of when a new animal year begins, and both are traditional.

The first is Li Chun (Beginning of Spring), one of the 24 solar terms. It marks the moment the sun's apparent ecliptic longitude reaches 315 degrees, usually around February 4 on the Gregorian calendar. A BaZi chart switches its year pillar at that moment. The second boundary is Chinese New Year, the first day of the first lunar month. Its Gregorian date shifts from year to year because the lunar months follow the new moon.

At the start of February 2027, both methods still use Bing Wu (丙午), the Horse year. Li Chun arrives on February 4, 2027, at 09:46 (Beijing time), when the BaZi year pillar becomes Ding Wei, the Goat. Chinese New Year falls on February 6, 2027. In Beijing, a baby born on the afternoon of February 4 or on February 5 is a Goat by the BaZi year pillar and a Horse by the lunar calendar. A baby born in Beijing on the morning of February 4, before Li Chun, is a Horse by both counts.

Some years the gap is much wider. In 2026, Li Chun came on February 4, 2026, at 04:02 (Beijing time), while Chinese New Year was February 17, 2026, nearly two weeks later. Anyone born in that stretch has a Horse year pillar but a Snake lunar year. If a relative has always called you one animal and a chart says another, check whether your birthday sits in this kind of window before assuming anything is wrong.

## Three harmony, six harmony and clash

Branches are traditionally said to relate to one another in fixed patterns. These are general rules about the symbols themselves. They describe how two branches sit on the circle, not what two people are like together.

Three harmony groups branches spaced four positions apart into one element: Shen, Zi and Chen form a water group; Hai, Mao and Wei form a wood group; Yin, Wu and Xu form a fire group; and Si, You and Chou form a metal group. In each group, the middle branch is the one where that element is traditionally strongest.

Six harmony links the branches in six fixed pairs: Zi with Chou, Yin with Hai, Mao with Xu, Chen with You, Si with Shen, and Wu with Wei. The Wu–Wei six harmony pairs the height of summer with the month just after it, and the Zi–Chou six harmony does the same for midwinter.

Clash sets each branch against the one directly opposite on the circle, six positions away. The Zi–Wu clash puts north against south, water against fire. The Chou–Wei clash sets the two yin earth branches against each other across the winter and summer sides of the year.

## Your animal is one branch of a BaZi chart

A BaZi chart, also called the Four Pillars, records the year, month, day and hour of birth. Each pillar has one of the ten Heavenly Stems on top and one of the twelve Earthly Branches underneath, for eight characters in all. Your zodiac animal is just one of them: the branch of the year pillar.

Traditional readings center on a different character, the Day Master, which is the stem of the day pillar. The year, month and hour pillars are read in relation to it. The month pillar places you within a solar-term month, and each branch also holds hidden stems that add more elements than the animal name shows. On top of that, luck pillars mark ten-year stretches that change how the chart is read across a lifetime. This is why two classmates born in the same Goat year can have charts with little in common.

## Chinese zodiac years and the 60-year cycle

Animals alone repeat every twelve years, but the full year name repeats every sixty. Ten stems paired with twelve branches, always odd with odd and even with even, produce sixty combinations. The Jia Zi (甲子) year of 1984 opened the most recent round, and the same pair will not come back until 2044.

Each pair also carries a Na Yin (納音) name, a poetic element label shared by two neighboring pairs. Jia Zi (1984) is Gold in the Sea. Bing Wu (2026) is Heavenly River Water, and Ding Wei (2027) is Heavenly River Water as well. Earlier Goat years show how much the names vary: Xin Wei (1991) is Earth by the Roadside, while Ji Wei (1979) is Fire in the Sky; the stems Xin (辛) and Ji (己) are what set them apart. Same animal, different stem, and a different image to reflect on.
