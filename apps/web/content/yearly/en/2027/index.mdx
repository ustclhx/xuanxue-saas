## 2027 Ding Wei: stem, branch and Na Yin

The sixty-year cycle pairs one of the ten Heavenly Stems with one of the twelve Earthly Branches. In 2027 the stem is Ding, the fourth stem, whose yin fire is often pictured as a candle or lamp flame. Wei is the eighth branch and belongs to earth. It corresponds to the last solar-term month of summer and the traditional double hour from 1 to 3 p.m. Together they form Ding Wei, the year commonly called the Fire Goat.

Wei has a few traditional roles worth knowing before you read the table. It is one of the four Earth storehouse branches, holding the remains of Wood; it closes the Wood group of three harmony formed by Hai (亥), Mao (卯) and Wei; and its hidden stems are Ji (己) Earth, Ding (丁) Fire and Yi (乙) Wood. In the solar-term calendar that BaZi uses for months, the Wei month runs from Minor Heat in July to Li Qiu (Beginning of Autumn) in August.

The Na Yin system gives each pair of adjacent stem-branch combinations a shared image. Ding Wei (2027) is Heavenly River Water, the same name that Bing Wu (2026) carries. So the element in the popular name, Fire, comes from the stem, the animal comes from the branch, and the Na Yin adds a third layer with its own element, Water. Keeping the three apart avoids most of the confusion around "the element of the year."

## The 12 animals and the Ding Wei year

The table below compares each animal's branch with Wei using the classical pair rules. Four animals receive a Tai Sui label in 2027. The Goat, whose branch is Wei itself, is meeting Tai Sui. The Ox is clashing with Tai Sui and punishing Tai Sui, because the Ox and Goat stand opposite each other on the wheel and also share the punishment group with the Dog; this is the Ox–Goat clash. The Dog is punishing Tai Sui and breaking Tai Sui. The Rat is harming Tai Sui, from the Zi–Wei harm.

Three animals receive harmony labels. The Horse's Wu (午) and the Goat's Wei form the Wu–Wei six harmony, one of six fixed pairs. The Rabbit and the Pig share the wood group with the Goat, as the Mao–Wei three harmony and Hai–Wei three harmony entries show. Each entry compares two members of the group. All three branches, Hai, Mao and Wei, are needed to make up the full group.

For the Tiger, Dragon, Snake, Monkey and Rooster, this comparison with Wei gives no harmony, clash, punishment, harm or break. These animals have no Tai Sui label for 2027. Their own pages also compare the five elements, which can show a connection even when this table lists no branch relation.

## Two start dates: Li Chun and Chinese New Year

There are two common ways to decide when the Goat year begins, and they give different answers for a few days in early 2027.

BaZi follows the solar terms. The annual pillar changes at Li Chun (Beginning of Spring), on February 4, 2027, at 09:46 (Beijing time). A person born at 08:00 Beijing time that morning still has Bing Wu, the Horse, as the year pillar; someone born two hours later has Ding Wei.

The lunar calendar counts from Chinese New Year, which falls on February 6, 2027. In Beijing, a birth on February 4 after Li Chun or on February 5 falls in the Goat year by BaZi and the Horse year by the lunar calendar. Family members may name a different animal when they count from the first day of the lunar year.

Li Chun occurs at the same instant worldwide. If a birth time is recorded elsewhere, convert it to Beijing time before comparing it with the time above. For example, 8 p.m. on February 3 in Los Angeles is noon on February 4 in Beijing. That is after Li Chun, so the BaZi year pillar is Ding Wei.

## Beyond the year branch

The animal is the year branch, one of the eight characters in a BaZi chart. The month, day and hour pillars have branches of their own. For example, a Tiger's year branch Yin (寅) has no listed relation with Wei, while a Horse branch in another pillar would form the Wu–Wei six harmony. Which chart branch you compare changes the result.

At the center of a BaZi reading is the Day Master, the stem of the day pillar. Comparing Ding Fire and the hidden stems of Wei with the Day Master gives the Ten Gods for the year, which describe roles such as resource, output or pressure. The current luck pillar, a ten-year period with its own stem and branch, adds a further layer.
