## Branch relations with the Ding Wei year

The Horse's branch is Wu (午), the seventh of the twelve Earthly Branches, set at due south on the traditional compass and matched with the fifth lunar month, the peak of summer. 2027 is a Ding Wei (丁未) year: Ding (丁) is the yin fire stem and Wei (未) is the earth branch that follows Wu directly. If you want the full sequence of branches and the years each one covers, the [Chinese zodiac years](/chinese-zodiac) page lays them out in order.

Between these two neighbors the classical tables record one relation: Wu–Wei six harmony. Six harmony pairs are fixed around the circle, and Wu with Wei is the pair that sits at the top of summer. The bond runs both ways, but the two sides are not in the same position this year. For someone born in a Goat year, Wei is their own branch, so the Goat is meeting Tai Sui (太歲) in 2027, a label the Horse does not carry. The [Year of the Goat 2027](/chinese-zodiac-2027/goat) page reads the same pair from the other side.

Read six harmony as a description in a rulebook, not as a promise about what the year contains. It tells you that, in the traditional system, these two branches are counted as a matched pair. It says nothing about your job title, your bank balance or your family. A useful way to hold it is as a question about fit: where in your life do two different rhythms already work together, and where have you been forcing a match?

Last year, 2026 (Bing Wu), the annual branch was Wu itself, so Horse-born readers spent a full year under their own branch. Moving from that year into a Wei year is a shift from repeating your own note to sitting beside a neighbor. One reflective prompt: name one commitment you took on during the Bing Wu year and decide, with whoever shares it, whether it continues into 2027 as it is, in a new form, or not at all.

## Reading 2027 with your full BaZi chart

Your animal comes from one character, the branch of your year pillar. A complete BaZi chart has eight characters across the Four Pillars of year, month, day and hour, and the stem of the day pillar, the Day Master, is the reference point for everything else. So the Wu–Wei six harmony describes only one line of comparison. To see how Ding and Wei sit against the rest of your chart, enter your birth data in [BaZi 2027](/bazi-2027) and look at which of your pillars the annual pillar touches.

Three things are worth checking. First, compare the Ding fire stem with your Day Master through the Ten Gods: the same Ding reads as a peer to a Bing or Ding day, as output to a Jia or Yi day, and as pressure to a Geng or Xin day.

Second, scan your month, day and hour branches for Chou, Xu or Zi, since a relation between Wei and one of those can matter as much as the year branch does. Third, note which of your luck pillars you are in, because a ten-year pillar sits underneath every annual pillar it covers.

Wei also carries hidden stems: Ji (己) earth as the main qi, with Ding (丁) fire and Yi (乙) wood stored inside. Wu itself holds Ding fire and Ji earth, so the two branches share two of the same hidden stems, which is part of why the pairing reads as close. If you want to compare your chart with another person's rather than with the year, [BaZi chart compatibility](/bazi-compatibility) sets two charts side by side.
