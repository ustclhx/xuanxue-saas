## Branch relations with the Ding Wei year

Si sits on the wheel just before the southern point, where spring has fully given way to summer. Wei sits just past that point, in the closing month of summer. Between them lies Wu (午), the Horse and the midsummer branch. Si and Wei share no classical pair rule. There is no clash, no harmony, no punishment, harm or break, so the 2027 table leaves the Snake's row blank and assigns no Tai Sui label.

What remains is the element picture. Si is Fire, and so is the year stem Ding; Wei is Earth. In the five-element cycle Fire generates Earth, so the Snake's branch is described as feeding the year branch. This is a statement about how elements are classified, not a prediction. The [Chinese zodiac](/chinese-zodiac) page lists each branch with its element and season if you want to see how the summer branches compare.

The Si–Shen six harmony pairs the Snake’s branch with the Monkey’s. The same pair also appears under punishment and break. You can read how Shen stands with Wei on the [Year of the Monkey 2027](/chinese-zodiac-2027/monkey) page.

A prompt to keep nearby: whose ground are you warming, and are you also keeping enough warmth for yourself?

## Reading 2027 with your full BaZi chart

A BaZi chart has eight characters: four stems and four branches. The Snake’s Si accounts for just one of them. If Chou appears in the month or day pillar, that position shows both the Chou–Wei clash and the Chou–Wei punishment. Zi would show the Zi–Wei harm, while Wu would show the Wu–Wei six harmony. Mark which pillar holds each of these branches before moving on to the stems.

Stems come next. Ding, the year stem, is yin Fire. Wei has Ji (己) Earth at its core and holds Ding Fire and Yi (乙) Wood as hidden stems. For a Fire Day Master, Ding counts as a peer and Yi Wood is a resource. For a Metal Day Master, Ding belongs to the element that controls Metal. The current luck pillar adds its own pair of characters to the comparison.
