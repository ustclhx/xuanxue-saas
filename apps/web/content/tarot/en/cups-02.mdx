## Overview and symbolism

Two figures face one another with cups held between them, creating a balanced exchange. The central arrangement draws attention to meeting rather than to either person in isolation. The Two of Cups explores connection and reciprocity. It asks whether an interaction allows both people to be present as themselves, with the ability to offer, receive, and decline freely.

## Keywords

- Upright: connection, reciprocity.
- Reversed: imbalance, disconnection.

## Upright meaning

Reciprocity does not require identical gestures. One person may initiate a conversation while the other makes time to listen, and both contributions can matter. Look at whether interest and care move in both directions over time. A meaningful connection leaves room for differences while maintaining a shared willingness to understand and respond.

## Reversed meaning

Imbalance and disconnection may appear when one person does most of the reaching out or when both assume their own way of showing care is obvious. Describe the pattern before assigning motives. A direct conversation can clarify expectations, although it cannot require the other person to want the same connection or offer more than they choose.

## Love

Ask what helps each of you feel heard and appreciated. Alongside attraction, pay attention to mutual consent and the ordinary behaviors that help you understand how the connection feels to each of you. Look for moments when both of you initiate contact, listen attentively, and follow through on a shared agreement.

## Career

A productive partnership needs clear expectations and a respectful exchange of skills. Discuss how contributions are recognized and how disagreement will be handled. If collaboration feels uneven, review responsibilities rather than relying on goodwill to compensate for an unclear arrangement.

## Advice

Think of one ongoing exchange and write what each person offers, receives, and needs clarified. Use that note to form a specific question. The purpose is to understand the relationship, not to keep a secret score of every gesture.

## FAQ

### Does the Two of Cups always refer to romantic love?

No. Its connection and reciprocity themes also apply to friendship, collaboration, and other mutually chosen relationships. The context of your question matters more than assuming every pair represents romance.

### What does Two of Cups reversed mean?

The reversed themes are imbalance, disconnection. Use them to examine the situation from another angle. A reversal can highlight a difficulty, an adjustment, or a constructive response. Choose the interpretation that fits your observations and the position in the spread.

### What question can I ask with Two of Cups?

Ask: "How does care or effort move in both directions in this connection?" Keep the answer connected to your own observations and choices. If the interpretation does not fit, you can set it aside rather than forcing your experience to match the card.
