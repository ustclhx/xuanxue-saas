## Overview and symbolism

A seated figure holds balanced scales in one hand and an upright sword in the other. The symmetrical arrangement gives the image a measured, deliberate quality. Justice invites attention to fairness, evidence, and the effects of decisions. The scales suggest comparison; the sword suggests the need to arrive at a clear distinction rather than leaving every question permanently unresolved.

## Keywords

- Upright: fairness, accountability.
- Reversed: bias, avoidance.

## Upright meaning

Reflect on fairness, accountability.

Accountability begins with describing what happened accurately, including your own contribution. Fairness asks whether the same standard is being applied consistently and whether relevant context has been heard. You can acknowledge an impact without turning the discussion into a verdict on someone's entire character. A proportionate response addresses the actual action and the repair it may require.

## Reversed meaning

Consider how bias, avoidance may shape your perspective.

Bias and avoidance can distort an assessment before a decision is made. Look for information you have dismissed because it complicates your preferred account. Avoidance may also involve postponing an apology or declining to state a standard clearly. The reversal invites a more honest process, not a declaration that you or another person are inherently unfair.

## Love

Compare expectations openly, especially around shared responsibilities and the effort of maintaining contact. Fairness does not always mean identical tasks; it means an arrangement both people can understand and discuss. Listen to the effect of your behavior before defending the intention behind it.

## Career

Use agreed criteria when reviewing work or dividing responsibilities. Document relevant facts and allow people to correct misunderstandings. This card is a reflection prompt about process and accountability; it cannot resolve legal questions or replace advice from a qualified legal professional.

## Advice

Describe a disagreement without loaded adjectives. Separate observations, interpretations, and requests. Then ask what a fair next step would be if you did not already know which side you preferred. That exercise can reveal a missing perspective.

## FAQ

### Can Justice predict a court decision?

No. Its imagery does not forecast legal outcomes or provide legal advice. For a legal matter, consult a qualified professional and use the card only to reflect on your own communication and accountability.

### What does Justice reversed mean?

The reversed themes are bias, avoidance. Use them to examine the situation from another angle. A reversal is not automatically negative, and it does not establish what someone else thinks or what will happen.

### What question can I ask with Justice?

Ask: "What evidence or perspective would make my assessment fairer?" Keep the answer connected to your own observations and choices. If the interpretation does not fit, you can set it aside rather than forcing your experience to match the card.
