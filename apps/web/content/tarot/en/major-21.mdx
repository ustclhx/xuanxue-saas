## Overview and symbolism

A central figure moves within an oval wreath, surrounded by four figures at the corners of the image. The frame creates a sense of wholeness while the movement prevents it from feeling static. The World explores completion and integration: a cycle can be recognized as finished even while its learning becomes part of whatever you do next.

## Keywords

- Upright: completion, integration.
- Reversed: unfinished work, delay.

## Upright meaning

Reflect on completion, integration.

Completion deserves a clear definition. You may have met the purpose of a project, reached the end of a course, or brought several strands of experience into a coherent understanding. Integration asks what you can carry forward. Take time to notice how the parts fit together instead of treating the finish only as permission to begin another obligation.

## Reversed meaning

Consider how unfinished work, delay may shape your perspective.

Unfinished work and delay may involve one practical loose end or difficulty accepting that something is sufficiently complete. Distinguish a real requirement from a moving standard of perfection. If a task remains, name it precisely. If the work is done, consider whether a closing conversation or simple acknowledgment would help you let the cycle end.

## Love

A shared milestone can be appreciated without making it a final definition of the relationship. Discuss what you have learned together and what each person now needs. Wholeness does not require a partner to supply every interest, friendship, or source of meaning in your life.

## Career

Close a project with an accessible handover, a record of decisions, and recognition of contributors. Integration means that useful knowledge does not disappear when the immediate task ends. The card does not promise career success; it invites a careful assessment of work already completed.

## Advice

Create a small completion checklist with only the actual requirements. Finish or assign any remaining item, then record one lesson worth keeping. Give the ending a visible marker so that it does not remain mentally open through habit alone.

## FAQ

### Does The World mean there is nothing left to learn?

No. Completion applies to a particular cycle, not to all experience. Integration means using what you have learned while remaining open to new questions and different contexts.

### What does The World reversed mean?

The reversed themes are unfinished work, delay. Use them to examine the situation from another angle. A reversal is not automatically negative, and it does not establish what someone else thinks or what will happen.

### What question can I ask with The World?

Ask: "What is complete, and what learning deserves to travel with me?" Keep the answer connected to your own observations and choices. If the interpretation does not fit, you can set it aside rather than forcing your experience to match the card.
