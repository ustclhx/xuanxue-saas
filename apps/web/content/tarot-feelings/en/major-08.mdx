## What Strength suggests about how someone feels

In the Strength card, a woman rests her hand on a lion's jaw, closing its mouth without any sign of struggle. Her touch looks almost casual. The lion is not beaten; it is calm. As a feelings card, that gentle hand describes emotion that is powerful underneath and handled with a lot of tenderness on the surface.

Someone whose feelings carry this quality may care intensely but not show the full force of it. They regulate it. That can read as calm or even restraint, when it is actually a choice not to overwhelm you.

## Upright: patience that holds a lot

Upright, the keywords are patience and courage. In behavior, this might look like someone who doesn't take it personally when you are slow to reply, who waits for you to be ready to talk about something hard, and who is brave enough to say "I like you" without needing an immediate answer back. It is a card of emotional maturity.

Courage is the less obvious half. Strength-style affection often includes small acts of nerve: admitting a mistake first, apologizing without defensiveness, standing up for you when you're not in the room.

The limit is that patience can be mistaken for indifference, and self-control can hide needs. Someone who always stays calm may not be telling you when they are hurt.

## Reversed: the lion gets restless

Reversed, the fixed keywords are self-doubt and impatience. The lion slips loose a little. In one direction, someone feels a lot but doubts they are worth your time, so they hesitate, overthink, or retreat before they can be rejected. In another, the emotion pushes for faster answers: repeated messages, frustration at mixed signals, a need to know now.

Neither is a reason for alarm. The reversed card points toward the same remedy: kinder self-talk, slower pacing, and more honest naming of what you feel.

## Strength as feelings from an ex

From an ex, Strength can describe someone handling the breakup with a lot of restraint. They may still feel strongly and choose not to reach out, out of respect for the ending. They may send one calm, kind message and then step back.

You might recognize this in the ex who congratulates you on a new job and asks for nothing in return, or who stays friendly at a mutual friend's party without reopening old arguments. That restraint can be a form of care, even when it feels like distance.

It can also describe your own resilience. The card does not suggest a return. It suggests that whatever remains can be carried with dignity.

## A crush that takes up a lot of room

Strength suits a crush that feels bigger than the situation warrants. You think about them more than you would like, you replay small moments, and you are trying not to let it run your week. The card's image speaks directly to that: the feeling is a lion, and you can rest a hand on it rather than letting it pull you around.

Being gentle with yourself is part of this. Strong attraction is not foolish. You can admit it, enjoy it, and still act with care.

## Holding the feeling gently

For seven days, notice when the feeling flares, and before you act on it, wait ten minutes. Then decide whether to send that message or make that plan. You are not suppressing anything. You are practicing the calm-handed patience the card describes, which makes whatever you do next more honest.
