## Seven of Cups: how a daydream feels

A dark silhouette stands with its back to us, facing seven cups floating in a bank of cloud. Each cup holds something different: a castle, a pile of jewels, a laurel wreath, a snake, a dragon, a curly-haired head, and in the center a glowing figure draped in a veil. Every option shines, and some are not what they seem.

That veiled figure in the middle says a lot about the feeling. Someone is attracted, but part of what they are drawn to is hidden, imagined or projected. The emotion is dreamy and full of possibility, with clarity still out of reach.

## Upright: many shining cups

Options and fantasy are the upright keywords. In behavior, it can look like big talk about futures that never get scheduled, long late-night conversations about “what if,” or someone keeping several possible connections open at once because they cannot choose. They may adore an idea of you more than the everyday reality.

The card leans maybe. The feeling is not fake; dreams are a real part of attraction. Its limit is that daydreams are not decisions. Until someone picks a cup and drinks from it, the possibilities stay floating.

## Reversed: the clouds part

Reversed, clarity and narrowing options appear. Someone stops daydreaming and starts choosing. They may become more decisive, more honest about what they want, or simply more grounded. It can feel like a fog lifting.

It leans maybe because clarity can point in any direction. Still, clear people are easier to understand, and even a disappointing answer is easier to live with than endless haze. You may notice them deleting apps, turning down other invitations, or speaking about the future in specific terms instead of sweeping ones. Those small signs of choosing are worth more than any grand speech.

## Seven of Cups as feelings from an ex

From an ex, this card often signals imagined versions of the past or future. They may remember the relationship as better or worse than it was, or picture a reunion that has little to do with the actual problems you faced. Their messages might feel vague, romantic or confusing.

A floating picture is not a plan. A dreamy message can be sincere and still carry no intention at all, and the image cannot sort the two. If you want clarity, ask a concrete question and see whether the answer lands on the ground.

## The idealized crush

This is the card of the crush you barely know. You have seen them across the room, read their posts, had two great conversations, and your mind has filled in the rest. The glowing veiled figure might be your own projection: someone shaped partly by your hopes.

There is nothing wrong with dreaming. It is part of the fun of liking someone. The kind thing for both of you is to let the real person step out from behind the veil, through ordinary time and conversation, before you decide who they are.

## Choosing one cup

List three things you believe about this person. Next to each, write whether you have seen it with your own eyes or imagined it. Then pick one imagined belief and test it gently, through a question or a plain, everyday plan. Real details, even unglamorous ones, are better ground than a beautiful cloud.
