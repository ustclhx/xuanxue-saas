## The Sun and feelings that don't hide

A small child rides a white horse with arms flung wide open, no hands on the reins, while tall sunflowers lean over a garden wall behind. Nothing in the picture is defended. As a feelings card, those open arms are the key: affection that is shown freely, without strategy.

The Sun describes some of the easiest feelings to read in tarot. Someone who feels this way tends to be visibly glad to see you. Their warmth is out in the open, sometimes almost childlike in its enthusiasm.

## Upright: warmth in plain view

Upright, the keywords are joy and clarity. In behavior, this might look like quick replies with jokes and enthusiasm, invitations to things they love, introductions to friends, and a general sense of lightness when you're together. There's little guessing. Even shy people show a Sun-like feeling through how much they smile.

Clarity is the gift here. Feelings like this tend to be straightforward to name, and conversations about where things stand often go smoothly.

The limit is depth. Joy is real, but a relationship also needs ways to handle the cloudy days. Notice how the brightness holds up when something goes wrong.

## Reversed: a cloud passes over

Reversed, the fixed keywords are muted joy and overconfidence. In one version, the warmth is still there but dimmed: someone is tired, stressed or distracted, and the playful energy has faded for now. In the other, a person is so sure of the connection that they start taking it for granted, stop making effort, or assume you feel exactly as they do.

Neither is a disaster. Both are a cue to check in, lightly and honestly.

Sometimes the overconfidence is your own. If a connection has felt easy for a while, it is tempting to stop asking questions because you assume you know the answers. A quick "how are you really feeling about us?" can keep the brightness from turning into assumption.

## The Sun as feelings from an ex

From an ex, The Sun often describes someone who has come out of the breakup feeling good, clearer and lighter, maybe even grateful. They might be warm when you cross paths, or speak well of the relationship.

That warmth can be a gift, and it can sting if you're still hurting. The card doesn't tell you what they'd want if you reached out. It does suggest the ending left room for goodwill.

## A crush that lights up your week

The Sun is a natural fit for a happy crush: the person whose name makes you grin, whose messages you reread, whose presence makes an ordinary Tuesday better. The card honors that joy without asking you to analyze it to death.

It also invites honesty. Sun-like feelings tend to show anyway, so you may be more obvious than you think, and that's fine. Notice whether they light up too when you're around. Do they seek you out in a group, remember the small things you mention, keep conversations going? Those everyday signals are more reliable than any single bright moment.

## Letting it be seen

Do one thing this week that shows your warmth plainly, without irony: tell someone you enjoy their company, invite them to something you love, or answer honestly when they ask how you are. Then pay attention to how it feels to be visible. That feeling is often the best clue to what you want next.
