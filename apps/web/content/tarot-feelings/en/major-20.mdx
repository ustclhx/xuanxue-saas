## Judgement and feelings that wake up

An angel blows a trumpet from the clouds, and below, people rise from open coffins with their arms stretched upward. Their faces turn toward the call. As a feelings card, those outstretched arms suggest a sudden awakening: something long dormant answering when it finally hears its name.

Judgement describes feelings that come back with new understanding. Someone may see a person or a past relationship clearly for the first time, and feel moved to respond.

## Upright: seeing it clearly and answering

Upright, the keywords are reflection and renewal. In behavior, this might look like an honest apology, a message that reflects real thought ("I've been thinking about how I treated you"), or a decision to commit after a long period of indecision. There's a sense of reckoning: owning what happened and wanting to begin again with that knowledge.

You may notice the change in specifics. Instead of a vague "sorry for everything," they name the moment they let you down. Instead of promising to be different, they describe what they've already done differently. Concrete reflection like this tends to be more trustworthy than sweeping statements.

This card often marks a turning point rather than a gentle drift. Feelings become clearer because someone has done some inner work.

The limit is that clarity on one side doesn't automatically meet readiness on the other. You're allowed to take your own time to respond.

## Reversed: hearing the call and looking away

Reversed, the fixed keywords are self-criticism and avoidance. Someone may know they need to face something but can't, perhaps because shame is too loud. They might stay away from you not out of indifference but because they're stuck in harsh judgment of themselves.

You may recognize this in your own reluctance to revisit the past. The reversed card asks for compassion, toward yourself and others, so that reflection can happen without punishment.

## Judgement as feelings from an ex

With an ex, Judgement often surfaces around a moment of reflection: an anniversary, a chance meeting, or simply the passage of enough time. An ex in this mode may be ready to acknowledge what went wrong. That can bring real closure, whether or not you reconnect.

The card cannot tell you whether a second chance is wise. It can help you check whether anything meaningful has changed, in them and in you. A reckoning that only exists in words, without any shift in behavior, is worth receiving kindly and holding loosely.

## A family member you're reconnecting with

Judgement fits a family reconnection especially well, such as a parent or sibling you've been distant from, or a relative reaching out after years apart. Families carry long histories, and reconnection often requires both people to look back honestly.

The card supports reflection and renewal without requiring you to forget anything. You can welcome contact while setting clear terms, and you can decide that some relationships stay at a distance. If past harm was serious, involving a therapist or a trusted mediator can make the conversation safer.

## Answering on your own terms

Write a short letter, not to send, describing what you would want acknowledged if you had a real conversation with this person. Then write a second letter describing what you'd want to acknowledge yourself. Read both after a few days. If you decide to reach out, those letters will help you say what matters most.
