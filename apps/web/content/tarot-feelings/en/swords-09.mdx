## Sleepless: what the Nine of Swords says about how they feel

Someone sits up in bed in the dark, face buried in both hands. Nine swords hang in horizontal rows on the black wall behind them. The quilt over their legs is patterned with roses and small symbols, and the side of the bed is carved with a scene of a fight.

This is the emotional state of lying awake with your thoughts. Someone shown through this card is caught in a loop of worry, replaying conversations and imagining outcomes. The swords are on the wall, not in the body: the pain is mostly in the mind, which does not make it any less real.

## Upright: overthinking in real time

People in a Nine of Swords mood often send late-night messages, then apologize for them in the morning. They may ask for reassurance more than usual, misread a short reply as anger, or bring up fears that seem to come from nowhere. Some go the other way and hide the anxiety entirely, appearing distant while their mind is racing.

The limit to keep in mind is that worry distorts. A person in this state may say they feel unloved or unwanted when what they mostly feel is scared. Responding to the fear underneath, rather than arguing with the words on top, usually goes further.

## Reversed: morning light through the window

Turned around, the night begins to lift. The card leans toward perspective and seeking support. Someone may finally tell a friend what has been keeping them up, ask you a direct question instead of guessing, or notice that the scenario they feared did not happen.

This shift is rarely instant. You might see a person who still has bad nights but recovers faster. That is progress, and it can be named kindly.

## The Nine of Swords as feelings from an ex

After a breakup, this card suggests rumination: going over what went wrong, rereading old messages, wondering what you are doing now. The feelings may include guilt, regret, or fear of having made a mistake.

What the card cannot tell you is whether that rumination leads them to reach out. Many people worry about an ex for months and never contact them. Their sleepless nights are theirs to work through. Yours deserve care too, whatever they decide.

## Long distance and the 2 a.m. spiral

Long-distance relationships are fertile ground for this card. Time zones mean one person is often awake while the other sleeps, and silence gets filled with imagination. A delayed reply turns into a story; a vague photo turns into a question.

If the Nine of Swords keeps appearing in a long-distance context, it suggests worry is filling the gaps that distance creates. Agreeing on a rhythm, such as a set call time or a morning message, can quiet a lot of that noise. So can saying, plainly, “I get anxious when I don’t hear from you, and I’d rather tell you than stew.”

## Putting the swords back on the wall

For the next seven nights, when you notice yourself spiraling, write the worry down in one line and leave it until morning. In daylight, decide whether it calls for a question, an action, or nothing at all. If anxiety starts to affect sleep or daily life most days, talk to a doctor or counselor. Support is part of the reversed card for a reason.
