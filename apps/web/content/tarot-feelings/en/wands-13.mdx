## Queen of Wands: warmth you can feel across a room

A queen sits on a throne carved with lions, a sunflower in one hand and a staff in the other. At her feet sits a black cat, watching the viewer directly. She looks relaxed and alert at once, as if she could host a party and read the room at the same time.

That cat is a good emotional clue. The warmth here is real and open, but it is not naive. Someone feels confident in their affection for you and comfortable showing it, while still seeing clearly what is going on.

## Upright: confident affection

Warmth and confidence are the upright keywords. In practice it looks like being greeted with a hug in front of everyone, getting invited to the birthday dinner, hearing someone say plainly, “I like spending time with you.” A person who feels this way rarely plays games. They laugh easily and share their life without making you work for it.

The card leans yes. Its limit is that confident people sometimes assume others feel just as secure. A Queen of Wands may move faster or speak more boldly than a shyer partner is ready for. Warmth works best when it also checks in.

## Reversed: a shadow on the sunflower

Reversed, jealousy and insecurity appear. The person may still feel strongly, but comparison creeps in. They notice who you text, who you mention, how long you stayed at the party. Sometimes it comes out as sharp humor, sometimes as quiet sulking.

Insecurity is not a character flaw; it often grows from past experiences, such as a partner who left without warning or a family where attention had to be competed for. Knowing that can make the prickly moments easier to meet with patience. Still, you are entitled to friendships and freedom, and jealousy that becomes controlling deserves a firm, kind line.

## Queen of Wands as feelings from an ex

From an ex, this card often suggests someone who remembers you fondly and has moved forward with their head up. They may be friendly when you meet, generous in how they speak about you, and genuinely glad when good things happen to you.

That openness has nothing to say about reunion one way or the other. Confident people can be warm toward an ex precisely because they feel settled in their new life. Take the kindness at face value. If seeing them so at ease stings, that is understandable; their confidence is not a measure of how much the relationship meant. You can be glad for their warmth and still give yourself time before being friends.

## A close friendship

This card describes the friend who throws your birthday party, introduces you to everyone and sends a voice memo the day of your interview. If the question is how a friend feels about you, the upright Queen points to loyal, sunny affection.

It can also describe a friendship that might be turning into something more. Warmth this strong sometimes blurs the line. If you suspect that is happening, it is kinder to ask than to guess, since a good friendship is worth protecting either way.

## Returning the warmth

This week, match the energy you are receiving with one specific gesture: plan something they would love, or tell them directly what you appreciate about them. Then notice how the exchange feels. Warmth that flows both ways tends to grow steadily; warmth that only flows one way can start to feel like hosting.
