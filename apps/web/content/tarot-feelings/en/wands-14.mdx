## The King of Wands and the feeling of having a plan

A king leans forward on a throne carved with lions and salamanders. At his feet, a small living salamander crawls across the ground, as if one of the carvings had come to life. He holds a flowering staff and looks off to the side, already thinking about the next move.

That leaning posture, and the little live salamander, say a lot about the emotion. The feeling is active and visionary. Someone feels strongly enough to make you part of a plan, and they would rather act on it than sit and wait.

## Upright: confident, decisive interest

With vision and leadership as keywords, the upright King often looks like commitment in action. A partner who talks about where you will live, sketches a five-year plan on a napkin, or confidently introduces you as their person. They take responsibility, keep their word and like to be the one who makes things happen.

It leans yes. Its limit is that leaders sometimes forget to consult. The plan may be generous and still not fully yours. Strong feelings that arrive as a blueprint need space for your edits.

## Reversed: the throne gets crowded

Reversed, domination and impatience take over, and the lean turns to no. Feelings can come out as control: choosing the restaurant every time, pressuring you to move faster, getting short when you disagree. Impatience may also show up as frustration that the relationship is not progressing on their schedule.

None of that cancels care, but it does crowd it. You get to keep your own voice, and a person who respects you will make room for it. If disagreeing with them starts to feel risky rather than merely uncomfortable, that is a sign to talk with someone you trust outside the relationship.

## King of Wands as feelings from an ex

An ex in this card may have already moved on to a new chapter with characteristic speed: a new job, a new city, a new plan. They may still feel respect or even affection for you, and could reach out confidently, perhaps with a proposal of some kind.

The decisiveness of that approach does not mean the old problems are solved. Ask what has changed and whether the plan includes your needs this time. A confident tone alone settles nothing.

## Feelings for a boss or senior coworker

This card speaks clearly to attraction toward someone with authority at work: a manager, a mentor, a team lead whose vision inspires you. Admiration for how someone leads can feel a lot like romantic interest, and sometimes it is both.

It may also describe how a senior colleague feels about you: they see potential, push you toward bigger projects and take a personal interest in your growth. That kind of investment is easy to read as romance when it may simply be mentorship. Power differences change everything in that setting. Feelings are not wrong, but acting on them carries real risks for both people. Workplace policies and the possibility of pressure deserve serious thought before anything personal happens.

## Bringing your own vision

Write down three things you want for your own life over the next year. Share one of them with this person and ask how it fits with their plans. If they light up and make room, the King’s vision may have space for you. If they talk past it, that is worth noticing before you sign up for anyone else’s blueprint.
