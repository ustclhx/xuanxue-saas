## Five of Cups: how disappointment feels

A figure wrapped head to toe in a black cloak stands with bowed head before three cups that have tipped over, their contents soaking into the ground. Behind the figure, two cups still stand upright, unnoticed. Farther back, a bridge crosses a river toward a small house.

The two standing cups make this card more than a picture of loss. Someone feels grief and regret strongly enough that they cannot turn around. What remains is real, and so is the bridge home, but in this moment their eyes are fixed on what spilled.

## Upright: mourning what spilled

Disappointment and grief are the upright keywords. In daily life, a person in this state might replay what went wrong, bring up the same regret more than once, go quiet when certain topics come up, or seem unable to enjoy plans that would usually delight them. Their feelings for you may be real yet muffled by sadness.

The card leans no, mostly because heavy feelings leave little room for new hope at the moment. That is not a judgment on the connection. Grief is a sign that something mattered, and it needs room before anything new can grow.

## Reversed: turning toward what remains

Reversed, acceptance and recovery appear. The figure starts to turn and see the two cups still standing. In practice: laughing again, making small plans, talking about the loss without the same sharp pain. Someone may begin to reach out more, or simply seem lighter.

This leans maybe, because recovery rarely moves in a straight line. A good week can be followed by a hard one. Kindness toward both the progress and the setbacks helps.

## Five of Cups as feelings from an ex

From an ex, this card often reflects genuine mourning. They may feel regret about how things ended, sadness about what they lost, or guilt about their part. Sometimes that grief comes out as a late-night message or a sad song shared without explanation.

Mourning a relationship does not mean wanting to return to it. Many people grieve something deeply and still know it had to end. Only their clear words, given over time, can tell you which is true here. Meanwhile, your own grief deserves the same patience. You do not have to manage their sadness as well as yours.

## Missing someone across the distance

This card captures the particular sadness of long distance: the ache after a visit ends, the empty side of the bed, the birthday celebrated over video. The spilled cups are the moments you miss; the standing ones are what you still have, like nightly calls and a planned reunion.

When the distance feels heavy, it is easy to see only the spilled cups. Naming what is still standing, out loud and together, often eases the ache a little. If the sadness stays constant for months, it is fair to talk honestly about whether the arrangement still works for both of you.

## Counting the standing cups

Write two short lists: what has been lost or disappointed you, and what is still here. Read the second list out loud once a day for a week. If you want to support someone else in this state, ask, “Do you want to talk about it, or do you want company without talking?” Both are gifts.
