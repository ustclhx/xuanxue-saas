## Ten of Wands and how it feels to carry too much

A man walks toward a distant house hunched over a bundle of ten staffs, his arms wrapped around them so tightly that the wood hides his face. He can barely see where he is going. The house is close, yet he moves slowly, every step an effort.

That hidden face is the emotional heart of the card. The person behind it might feel a great deal, but their feelings are buried under obligations. When people say someone is “hard to read,” sometimes they mean someone who is simply too loaded to look up.

## Upright: love that is short on room

Burden and responsibility are the upright keywords. You might see someone who cancels dates because of deadlines, falls asleep during a movie, answers messages three days later with a sincere apology. They may be the one everyone else relies on: the dependable sibling, the manager who covers every shift, the friend who never says no.

The card leans no for a quick answer, but the reason matters. It is not necessarily that they do not care. It is that there is no space left on their arms for anything new. Romance needs some slack, and this person has none at the moment.

## Reversed: setting a few staffs down

Reversed, release and delegation appear. Here the person begins to unload: they turn down an extra project, hire help, tell family they cannot fix everything. This can make room for feelings that were there all along, and you may notice them becoming more present, more playful, more available.

It is a shift worth encouraging. Lean toward maybe, and give it a little time to settle into new habits rather than a single good week.

## Ten of Wands as feelings from an ex

An ex in this card may look back on the relationship as one more thing they could not carry well. They might feel guilt, tiredness or relief, or all three at once. If they reach out, it may be to apologize for being absent during a stressful season.

An apology like that can be meaningful, yet the bundle of staffs says nothing about whether they want to pick the relationship back up. Sometimes setting a burden down is exactly what they needed, and the kindest thing is to let that be true.

## Feelings for a coworker

At work, this card is very literal. It can describe how you feel about a colleague who is drowning in tasks, or how a coworker you are drawn to keeps everyone at arm’s length because they have too much on their desk. Attraction in a high-pressure team often hides behind efficiency and short replies.

It can also point to resentment: the feeling that one person always ends up carrying the team. If you are the one carrying it, that is worth naming at work, separate from any personal feelings. Mixing the two tends to blur both: a crush can make you take on extra tasks to stay close, and resentment can make you read a colleague’s tiredness as coldness. Keeping them apart in your own mind makes each easier to handle honestly.

## Making room

This week, ask the person, or yourself, a practical question: “What is one thing you could put down this month?” Then offer one concrete form of help that costs you little, like taking care of a small errand. Notice whether lightening the load changes how present they can be.
