## Three of Wands and the feeling of looking ahead

Seen from behind, a figure in a red and green robe stands on a cliff with three staffs planted in the ground, watching ships move across a golden sea. We never see the face. The whole card is posture: steady, attentive, turned toward something on its way.

That turned back tells you a lot. The feeling here is anticipation. Someone feels hopeful about what this connection could become and is willing to wait for the ships to come in, at least for now. It is interest with a horizon attached.

## Upright: hope with a plan

Upright, the card carries expansion and foresight. Someone who feels this way tends to think in next steps. They might research flights before you mention visiting, talk about a future job in your city, or send you photos of places they want to show you. Their affection has room in it, and it likes to grow.

The limit is distance, literal or emotional. Foresight lives partly in the imagination, and a person can love the idea of where things are heading while being less present in the day-to-day. The upright lean is yes, but it is a yes to building, not a yes that has already been built.

## Reversed: ships that are late

Reversed, the keywords are setbacks and impatience. Plans slip, a visa or a job falls through, the timing is wrong. The feeling can turn tense: short replies, frustration that leaks into conversations, or a sudden “maybe this isn’t working” after a single disappointment.

That impatience usually says more about the strain than about the strength of the bond. Setbacks test whether two people can adjust together, and that is worth watching kindly.

## Three of Wands as feelings from an ex

An ex in this card is often looking outward. They may be planning a move, a new role or a fresh start, and you may come up as part of looking back before they go. Messages might sound nostalgic yet strangely forward-focused.

What the card cannot tell you is whether their horizon includes you. Looking toward the sea means they are focused on what is coming, and you do not know what is on the ships. Let their plans speak for themselves.

## Long distance

This is one of the clearest long-distance cards in the deck. The cliff, the water, the waiting: it describes two people who invest in a future they cannot fully touch yet. Video calls scheduled across time zones, a countdown to the next visit, a shared spreadsheet for saving toward a move.

Long distance works when the hope is shared and concrete. If only one person is watching the horizon, the waiting can quietly become lonely. Pay attention to who initiates the calls, who brings up the next visit, and who adjusts their schedule when time zones get awkward. Effort that flows in both directions is the healthiest version of this card; effort that always flows one way deserves a gentle conversation.

## A question for the horizon

Ask, with no pressure in your voice, “What does the next year look like for us in practical terms?” Then notice what follows over the next month. Do dates get set, do costs get discussed, does anyone move a piece of their life? Concrete steps are the ships coming in.
