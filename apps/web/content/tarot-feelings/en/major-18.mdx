## How The Moon colors what someone feels

A dog and a wolf howl up at the moon from either side of a winding path, and from a pool in the foreground a crayfish is crawling out onto land. Everything in the image is half lit. As a feelings card, that crawling creature suggests emotion rising from somewhere deep, before anyone has words for it.

The Moon describes feelings that are real but hard to read. Attraction may come mixed with uncertainty, longing with suspicion, closeness with fear. Often the person feeling them is as confused as you are.

## Upright: mixed signals in soft light

Upright, the keywords are uncertainty and imagination. In practice, this might look like a connection full of hints: replies that feel flirty one day and distant the next, late-night conversations that feel deep and then aren't mentioned, plans that stay vague. Imagination runs in to fill the gaps, on both sides.

That imaginative quality can be lovely. The Moon often comes with dreaminess, creativity and intense emotional atmosphere.

The limit is clarity. Imagination is a poor substitute for information. The card asks you to notice how much of your picture of this person is made of facts and how much of projections.

## Reversed: the fog starts to lift

Reversed, the fixed keywords are clarification and unease. Something is coming into focus. Someone may be ready to say what they really feel, or a hidden issue may come into the open. Reversed Moon feelings can be uncomfortable because clarity isn't always welcome, but they often bring relief.

Unease is the other half. You may sense something off before you can name it. That intuition deserves attention, without leaping to the worst conclusion.

## The Moon as feelings from an ex

With an ex, The Moon frequently reflects confusion. You may not know whether the occasional message means anything, and they may not know either. Memories can get rosier or darker with time, which makes it hard to trust your own sense of the past.

This is also the card of the 2 a.m. scroll through old photos, when the past looks softer than it was. Night thoughts about an ex are often louder and less reliable than daytime ones, so it can help to wait until morning before acting on them.

The card cannot sort fact from fantasy for you. It does suggest that what you imagine they feel may say as much about your own heart as theirs.

## Reading your own heart

The Moon is perhaps most useful as a mirror for your own feelings. When you're unsure how someone feels, it's easy to project your hopes or fears onto them. The card asks you to check which feelings are actually yours: Are you attracted to the person or to the mystery? Are you anxious because of what they did, or because of past experiences?

Untangling that won't tell you what they think. It will tell you what you need, which is the part you can act on.

## Separating facts from stories

Take a sheet of paper and draw a line down the middle. On the left, write only what this person has actually said or done. On the right, write what you've imagined or assumed. Read both sides. If the right side is much longer, choose one assumption to check gently, by asking rather than guessing.
