## The Page of Swords and feelings that start as questions

A young figure stands on a windy hilltop, holding a sword upright with both hands. Clouds race across the sky, a flock of birds scatters overhead, and the Page’s head is turned as if they just heard something behind them.

That alert, turned head is the emotional signature. Someone shown through this card is curious and watchful. Their feelings are bound up with wanting to know more: about you, about what is going on, about what everyone else thinks. There is energy here, but it is mostly mental, and it can jump from topic to topic like the wind on that hill.

## Upright: lots of questions, quick replies

In everyday terms, upright Page of Swords feelings look like attentive texting. Someone asks follow-up questions, remembers small details, sends you an article because it reminded them of something you said. They might be a little awkward or blunt, but they are engaged.

The limit is that curiosity is not commitment. A person can be fascinated by you and still be unsure about what they want. If the questions only ever flow one way, it is fair to notice that, too.

## Reversed: talk outruns truth

Flipped, the Page’s quickness turns into gossip and haste. You may notice someone repeating what they heard about you, sharing details of your relationship too freely, or firing off a reply before they have read your message properly.

Usually this comes from nerves, not malice. Anxious people sometimes talk to everyone except the person they need to talk to. A calm, direct check-in, “I heard something secondhand and would rather hear it from you,” tends to cut through the noise.

## The Page of Swords as feelings from an ex

From an ex, this card suggests curiosity that has not switched off. They may watch your stories, ask mutual friends how you are, or send a light message that feels like a probe. They want to know what you are up to.

What the card cannot tell you is why. Curiosity can come from missing you, from wanting reassurance that they made the right call, or simply from habit. It is information about their attention, not their intentions. If their checking in unsettles you, muting or limiting what they can see is a reasonable boundary, not a hostile one.

## A crush that is still a puzzle

This Page is at home in the early days of a crush, when every interaction feels like a clue. Maybe you are the Page: studying their schedule, rereading a message for subtext, asking friends what they think it meant.

The card does not discourage interest. It suggests sending some of that curiosity directly toward the person instead of around them. A real question asked face to face will tell you more than ten rounds of analysis with a friend.

## From watching to asking

Choose one thing you have been trying to work out about this person and ask it directly within the next week. Keep it light: “What do you like to do when you’re not working?” or “Would you want to get coffee sometime?” Also notice how much you share about the connection with others before you share it with them.
