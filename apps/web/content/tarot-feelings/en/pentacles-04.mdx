## Holding on: what the Four of Pentacles says about feelings

A crowned figure sits on a stone seat outside a city, hugging a pentacle tightly against his chest. Another pentacle balances on his crown, and his feet rest firmly on two more. He is not going anywhere, and neither is anything he owns.

The emotional state is guarded security. Someone shown through this card feels safest when they are in control of what matters to them. Feelings are present, but they are held close, protected, maybe rationed. The city at his back hints that he has chosen this posture even with life going on behind him.

## Upright: careful and protective

In practice, upright Four of Pentacles feelings can look like slow trust. Someone might not share much about their past, avoid saying “I love you” early, or keep parts of their life separate. They may show care through stability instead: being reliable, saving for shared plans, offering a steady presence.

There is real value in that. The limit is that holding too tightly can block closeness. A partner who never lets you in may be protecting themselves from loss in a way that also keeps love at a distance.

## Reversed: grip and release

Reversed, the card leans two ways, and both matter. One is possessiveness: clinging, jealousy, wanting to control where you go and who you see. The other is release: loosening the grip, sharing more, or letting go of something that no longer fits.

Look at behavior to tell which is happening. Release tends to feel like relief and openness. Possessiveness tends to feel like pressure. If you ever feel monitored or restricted in ways that scare you, reaching out to a trusted friend or a support line is wise.

## The Four of Pentacles as feelings from an ex

An ex shown through this card may still be holding on: to your hoodie, to old photos, to a sense that what you had was theirs. They might keep light contact without moving toward or away from you, simply because letting go feels like loss.

The card does not reveal whether that means they want you back. Sometimes people hold on to the past because it is familiar, not because they want to repeat it. If their holding on keeps you stuck, you can draw your own line.

## Your own guarded heart

Turned inward, this card asks how tightly you hold your own feelings. Are you keeping someone at arm’s length because opening up feels risky? Are you guarding your time, your space, or your heart so carefully that nobody can get close?

That caution often has good reasons behind it. The card does not shame it. It simply invites you to notice which pentacle you might be ready to set down. Maybe it is the rule that you never text first, or the habit of keeping plans vague so nobody can disappoint you.

## Loosening the grip a little

Pick one thing you usually keep to yourself in this connection, a worry, a hope, a preference, and share it with the other person this week. Keep it small. Notice what happens in your body as you do it, and how they respond. Both are useful information about whether this relationship can hold more trust.
