## How the Knight of Wands feels about you

A knight in a tunic covered with salamanders rides a horse that rears up on its hind legs, front hooves pawing the air. Flames seem to stream from his helmet plume and from the frayed edges of his sleeves. He holds his staff loosely, already half turned toward the next thing.

Emotionally, the rearing horse is the giveaway. Someone feels charged and eager, maybe even reckless with it. They want you now, they want adventure with you, and sitting still is the hardest thing in the world for them.

## Upright: passion at full speed

Adventure and enthusiasm are the upright keywords. In real life: a spontaneous road trip suggested on a Tuesday, flirting that is bold and confident, an invitation to meet their friends at a concert tonight. They pursue openly and enjoy the chase.

The card leans yes, and the attraction is usually sincere. The limit is that fire burns fastest when it has the most air. This kind of feeling may not yet know how to handle routine, slow weeks or the boring logistics that relationships eventually require.

## Reversed: all spark, no follow-through

Reversed, impulsiveness and inconsistency take over, and the lean shifts to no. The pattern is familiar: intense attention, then sudden absence. Big promises made at midnight, quietly forgotten by Friday. They may genuinely feel it while it lasts, which makes the drop-off confusing.

Protect your calendar. You can enjoy someone’s energy without rearranging your life around it. Keep the plans you made with friends, let replies wait until you actually have time, and notice how you feel on the quiet days. If the silences leave you anxious more often than the good days leave you happy, that balance is telling you something important.

## Knight of Wands as feelings from an ex

An ex in this card might come back the way they left: quickly. A burst of messages, a dramatic “I can’t stop thinking about you,” an invitation to meet tonight. The emotion can be real in the moment.

Yet a burst of heat reveals little about staying power, and the knight’s gallop gives no clue about where he stops. If they return in a rush, it is reasonable to slow things to your pace and see whether the interest survives a week of calm.

## The hot-and-cold situationship

This card captures a very particular kind of situationship: thrilling when it is on, silent when it is off. You might see each other three nights in a row, then hear nothing for ten days. Neither of you has named what it is, partly because the other person seems to like it that way.

Ask whether the excitement is worth the uncertainty for you. Some people enjoy a loose, adventurous arrangement for a while. Others find that the off weeks quietly drain the joy of the on weeks. Neither preference is wrong, but it helps to know which one is yours before the next burst of attention arrives and sweeps the question aside.

## Setting the pace yourself

Before your next plan together, decide what pace feels good to you: how often you want to see each other, how quickly you want things to move. Say it plainly once. Then watch over the next few weeks whether they adjust. Someone who cares tends to slow down for you; someone who only likes the chase tends to ride on.
