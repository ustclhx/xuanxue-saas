## The Hermit's lantern and how feelings travel

The Hermit stands alone on a snowy peak and holds out a lantern. Inside it glows a six-pointed star, and he carries it low, lighting just the next few steps. As feelings, that lantern describes care that is quiet and careful. It does not flood the room with light; it illuminates a little at a time.

Someone whose feelings have this quality is often thinking hard about what they feel. They may not text much. When they do talk, it may be long, honest and a little unexpected.

## Upright: closeness at a slower pace

Upright, the keywords are solitude and insight. Feelings under this card usually come with a need for space. The person may cancel plans to recharge, prefer a walk to a party, and share things one-on-one that they never post anywhere. When they open up, the insight can be striking.

What this looks like from your side is uneven contact paired with real depth. That can be wonderful if you like slow connection and tough if you need regular reassurance.

The limit is that reflection can go on a long time. Space is healthy; indefinite space is not a relationship.

It helps to ask what kind of contact actually nourishes a Hermit-like person. For many, a single long phone call on Sunday means more than fifty short messages during the week. Matching that rhythm, if it suits you, can turn their quiet into something you both enjoy.

## Reversed: the lantern goes dark

Reversed, the keywords are isolation and withdrawal. The healthy solitude of the upright card turns into shutting people out. Someone may go silent without explanation, stop responding to friends, or seem flat and unreachable. Often this has much more to do with their inner state than with anything you did.

If the reversed Hermit describes you, it is worth asking whether you are resting or hiding. If it describes someone you care about and you are worried, a short, warm message that asks nothing of them can keep a door open.

## The Hermit as feelings from an ex

After a breakup, The Hermit often appears when an ex has gone inward. They might have stepped back from social media, stopped coming to shared events, or simply gone quiet. That silence may be grief, reflection or relief, and the card does not specify which.

What it does suggest is that this is a time for space on their side. Pushing into it rarely brings the closeness you hope for.

## When a close friend pulls away

The Hermit is a strong lens for friendship, especially a friend who has slowly drifted into their own world. You used to talk every day; now they reply days later, if at all. It is easy to read that as rejection.

The card offers another reading: people sometimes need to withdraw to sort themselves out, and friendships can survive long quiet stretches if the door is left open. It also gives you permission to say you miss them, honestly, once.

## Leaving a light on

Write one message that offers presence without pressure. Something like: "No need to reply, just thinking of you and here when you want to talk." Then let it be. Over the next week, notice whether giving space feels peaceful or anxious for you, and treat that as information about your own needs.
