## The Six of Wands and feeling proud to be with someone

A rider on a white horse moves through a crowd, a laurel wreath on his head and a second wreath tied to the top of his staff. People on foot raise their own staffs around him. It is a homecoming parade, small but sincere, and the rider sits up straight.

As feelings, that wreath stands for pride. Someone feels good about themselves when they are with you, or good about you in a way they want others to notice. There is confidence here, and a pleasant glow of being admired or admiring.

## Upright: wanting the world to see

Recognition and confidence are the upright keywords. In daily life the feeling looks public. A partner introduces you with a grin, tags you in a photo the first week, tells their coworkers about the thing you accomplished. They celebrate your wins out loud, and they like it when you celebrate theirs.

This is a lovely energy, and the card leans yes. Its limit is that pride can be performed. Public praise is easy to give and easy to enjoy; the more telling test is how someone treats you when nobody is watching and nothing needs to be shown.

## Reversed: the crowd goes quiet

Reversed, ego and overlooked effort enter. One version is a person who cares more about how the relationship looks than how it feels. The other, more common version is someone who feels unseen: they plan the dinners, remember the birthdays, smooth over the hard days, and nobody seems to notice.

Both are worth taking seriously. Hurt pride often hides real tiredness, and a little recognition can soften a lot of defensiveness. If you feel this way yourself, it is fair to say so.

## Six of Wands as feelings from an ex

From an ex, this card often involves image. They may care how the breakup looks to friends, want to be seen as the one who is thriving, or feel a flicker of pride when they hear you are doing well. Sometimes it shows up as a congratulations message after your good news.

Nothing in the parade reveals whether that pride carries any wish to reconnect. Applause from the sidelines is a gesture of goodwill, and it can be received simply as that. If you want more from it, you will need more than a card to know whether it is there.

## Your own feelings about someone

This card is especially useful turned toward yourself. Ask what part of your attraction is about the person, and what part is about how being chosen by them makes you feel. Dating someone admired, successful or attractive can give a real boost, and there is nothing shameful in enjoying it.

The question is what remains when the boost fades. Do you like how they listen, how they handle a bad day, how they treat a waiter? Those private qualities are the part of the feeling that lasts beyond the parade.

## A week of noticing

For the next seven days, write down one moment when you felt proud of this person and one moment when you felt proud to be seen with them. Compare the lists at the end of the week. If both columns have entries, the feeling likely has depth. If only the second does, that is worth reflecting on gently, and without judgment.
