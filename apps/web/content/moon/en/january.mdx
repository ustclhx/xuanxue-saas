## Where the name comes from

The name Wolf Moon reached most readers through North American almanacs, especially the Maine Farmers' Almanac in the 1930s and later The Old Farmer's Almanac. Those publications assembled a list of monthly names from Native American, Colonial American and European folk sources, and January's entry became the one people now type into search bars after the holidays.

Almanacs explain the name with the sound of wolves carrying across frozen country in midwinter. Whether or not the howling was really louder in January, the image stuck because it matched the season: deep snow, early darkness and a bright moon over an empty landscape. The name records how people described the month, not anything the Moon does to the animals.

Older alternatives are still easy to find. The Old Moon reflects its place right after the turn of the year, and the Ice Moon speaks for itself in places where rivers and ponds lock up. Some older English lists also call it the Moon after Yule, a nod to the midwinter festival just past. You can check whether a Wolf Moon is up on our [full moon tonight](/full-moon) page.

## Viewing tips

January gives northern observers one of the best seats of the year. Because the full moon lies roughly opposite the Sun, and the winter Sun stays low, the Wolf Moon arcs high overhead near midnight. It spends many hours above the horizon, so you can catch it early in the evening, late at night or before dawn without searching.

Cold, dry air behind a passing front is often strikingly transparent. Look for the Moon rising in the east-northeast shortly after sunset on the night of the full moon, when it may look unusually large next to rooftops and trees. That is the moon illusion, a trick of perception rather than a real change in size. Our list of [full moon dates](/full-moon-calendar) shows each full moon this year in your time zone.

For detail, skip the full moon itself. In the evenings a few days after new moon, a slim crescent hangs in the southwest twilight, and the dark part of the disk often glows faintly with earthshine, sunlight bounced off Earth's snow and cloud. Around first quarter, binoculars reveal craters along the line between light and shadow. To see where the Moon is in its cycle tonight, open [moon phase today](/moon-phase-today).

Dress for the cold more than you think you need, and let your eyes adjust for a few minutes away from porch lights. In the southern hemisphere January is midsummer, so the full moon there stays low and the lit side of the crescent appears on the opposite edge.
