## Where the name comes from

Wolf Moon is the January name in The Old Farmer’s Almanac. The English names familiar today come from almanac lists that bring together terms from different places and traditions. Indigenous lunar calendars varied by community and region, and their months did not line up neatly with January through December.

Almanacs explain the name with the sound of wolves carrying across frozen country in midwinter. The association is with the time of year. Wolves howl to communicate with their pack and mark territory, among other reasons.

Farmers’ Almanac lists Old Moon and Ice Moon for January. Moon after Yule is a seasonal name that places a full moon after the midwinter festival. Which calendar month it falls in depends on when the Moon reaches full. You can check whether a Wolf Moon is up on our [full moon tonight](/full-moon) page.

## Viewing tips

At mid-northern latitudes, January’s full moon is usually high in the sky. Because the full moon lies roughly opposite the Sun, and the winter Sun stays low, the full moon crosses high in the southern sky around midnight. It spends many hours above the horizon, so you can often see it in the evening and again before dawn.

Cold, dry air behind a passing front is often strikingly transparent. Look for the Moon rising in the east-northeast around sunset on evenings near the full moon, when it may look unusually large next to rooftops and trees. That is the moon illusion, a trick of perception rather than a real change in size. Our list of [full moon dates](/full-moon-calendar) shows each full moon this year in your time zone.

For detail, skip the full moon itself. In the evenings a few days after new moon, a slim crescent hangs low in the western twilight, and the dark part of the disk often glows faintly with earthshine, sunlight reflected by Earth. Around first quarter, binoculars reveal craters along the line between light and shadow. To see where the Moon is in its cycle tonight, open [moon phase today](/moon-phase-today).

Dress for the cold more than you think you need, and let your eyes adjust for a few minutes away from porch lights. In the southern hemisphere January is midsummer, so the full moon there stays low and the crescent often looks rotated compared with a northern view.
