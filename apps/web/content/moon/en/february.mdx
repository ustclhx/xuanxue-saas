## Where the name comes from

The Snow Moon entered popular use through the same North American almanac tradition behind most modern moon names. When the Maine Farmers' Almanac began printing these labels in the 1930s, and The Old Farmer's Almanac carried them forward, February's choice drew on a plain observation: this is when snow piles deepest in many northern regions.

The best-known alternative is the Hunger Moon. Almanacs explain it as a reminder of how hard late winter could be for people living off stored food, with autumn supplies running thin and spring still weeks away. It is a historical note about scarcity, not a forecast. Some lists also mention a Storm Moon for the same month.

Because these names follow the calendar month, February is the one name that can go unused. When the cycle skips the month, almanacs simply move on to March. ## Viewing tips

The February full moon still runs high for northern observers, only slightly lower than in January. Since the full moon sits close to the point opposite the Sun, and the late-winter Sun is still low at noon, the Snow Moon climbs well up the southern sky around midnight. Over fresh snow, its light is bright enough to cast sharp blue shadows from trees and fences.

A snowy field under a full moon is one of the easiest winter scenes to enjoy without any equipment. Step outside an hour or two after moonrise, when the Moon has cleared the haze near the horizon, and notice how far you can see across open ground. Check our [next full moon](/full-moon) page for the exact rise and peak times where you live.

February is also a strong month for the opposite end of the cycle. In the evenings just after new moon, the young crescent stands steeply above the western horizon after sunset, which makes it easier to catch than at most other times of year. Look for the faint gray glow of earthshine on the unlit part of the disk; a pair of binoculars brings it out clearly.

If clouds hide the full moon, the waning phases offer another chance in the hours before dawn. A last quarter Moon rises around midnight and is high in the south at sunrise. You can follow the cycle night by night with [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today). South of the equator it is late summer, and the full moon there keeps to a lower, shorter path.
