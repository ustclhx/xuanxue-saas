## Where the name comes from

Snow Moon appears in Jonathan Carver’s eighteenth-century account of Indigenous lunar months in the interior of North America. He linked the name to heavy snowfall at that time of year. The Old Farmer’s Almanac uses Snow Moon for February in its modern calendar.

The Old Farmer’s Almanac lists the Cherokee name Hungry Moon, a reminder of how scarce food could be in late winter. Farmers’ Almanac also lists the variants Hunger Moon and Storm Moon. These names reflect the conditions people associated with the season.

Because these names follow the calendar month, February is the one name that can go unused. When the cycle skips the month, almanacs simply move on to March. Our [full moon calendar](/full-moon-calendar) lists every full moon in the year, so you can see at a glance whether this February has a Snow Moon.

## Viewing tips

The February full moon often takes a high path at mid-northern latitudes. Since the full moon sits close to the point opposite the Sun, and the late-winter Sun is still low at noon, the Snow Moon climbs well up the southern sky around midnight. Over fresh snow, its light is bright enough to cast sharp blue shadows from trees and fences.

A snowy field under a full moon is one of the easiest winter scenes to enjoy without any equipment. Step outside an hour or two after moonrise, when the Moon has cleared the haze near the horizon, and notice how far you can see across open ground. Check the date of the [next full moon](/full-moon), then look up moonrise for your location.

February is also a strong month for the opposite end of the cycle. In the evenings just after new moon, the young crescent stands steeply above the western horizon after sunset, which makes it easier to catch than at most other times of year. Look for the faint gray glow of earthshine on the unlit part of the disk; a pair of binoculars brings it out clearly.

If clouds hide the full moon, the waning phases offer another chance in the hours before dawn. A last quarter Moon rises around midnight and is high in the south at sunrise. You can follow the cycle night by night with [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today). South of the equator it is late summer, and the full moon there keeps to a lower, shorter path.
