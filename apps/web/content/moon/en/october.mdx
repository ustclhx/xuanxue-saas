## Where the name comes from

The Hunter's Moon is one of the older names in the English list, with European roots as well as a place in North American almanacs. Publications such as The Old Farmer's Almanac and the Maine Farmers' Almanac popularized the modern set of month names, drawing on Native American, Colonial American and European folk traditions.

The name is usually explained through the hunting season. Once fields had been cleared after the harvest, animals moving across the stubble were easier to see, and the bright evening moonlight let hunters keep tracking game after sunset. Stores of meat were then prepared ahead of the long winter. The name describes that history; it is not a reference to anything the Moon itself does.

The Hunter's Moon is defined by its place in a sequence: it is the full moon after the Harvest Moon. Some lists also give the October full moon names such as Travel Moon or Dying Grass Moon, both pointing to a landscape turning brown and people preparing for colder months. The Hunter's Moon name itself has a long history in English usage, older than the modern almanac lists.

## Viewing tips

Mid-autumn is a comfortable time to watch the Moon. Nights are long, evenings are cool without being bitter, and the air is often clearer than in the summer haze. The October full moon rises in the east around sunset and climbs higher than the summer full moons did, since the Sun is now lower at midday.

Like its September neighbor, the Hunter's Moon benefits from the shallow angle the Moon's path makes with the evening horizon at mid-northern latitudes. On the nights around full, moonrise comes only modestly later each evening. Pick a spot with an open eastern view and watch the disk lift through the trees; the low-angle light often gives it an amber color that fades to white as it climbs.

Bright autumn leaves and bare branches make good foreground for photographs. A tripod and a longer lens help keep the Moon large in the frame against the trees. Shoot while it is still low, because the sky is not yet fully dark and both foreground and Moon can be exposed well. South of the equator October is mid-spring, so the full moon there runs lower and the seasonal names do not follow the same story.
