## Where the name comes from

Sturgeon Moon appears in Jonathan Carver’s eighteenth-century account of Indigenous lunar months in the interior of North America. The Old Farmer’s Almanac uses the name for August and associates it with lake sturgeon caught in the Great Lakes and Lake Champlain.

Almanacs explain the August name through the lake sturgeon, a heavy, slow-growing fish that can live for many decades. In late summer it was widely caught in the Great Lakes and in waters such as Lake Champlain. A full moon named after the fish marked a time of year when it was a familiar sight in those waters. The fish is easy to recognize, with rows of bony plates along its sides and whisker-like barbels under its snout.

Alternative names lean toward fields rather than water. Green Corn Moon and Grain Moon both refer to late-summer crops. Together the names show a season of transition: high summer is fading, and the heavier harvest work of autumn is close.

## Viewing tips

In August the full moon is often still fairly low at mid-northern latitudes, although the seasonal trend is toward higher full moons as fall approaches. That is because the Sun is slowly dropping from its summer peak, and the full moon always sits roughly opposite it. Expect moonrise in the east-southeast around sunset, with a modest arc across the southern sky.

Nights are lengthening, which gives you a longer window after dark. Warm, still evenings are pleasant for sitting outside, though summer haze can dull the contrast. Binoculars on a steady support show the dark lunar seas and bright rayed craters well; look along the terminator, the boundary between lunar day and night, to see shadows cast by crater rims. The quarters are especially good for this because the boundary runs across the middle of the visible disk.

This month also brings the Perseid meteor shower. Check the [full moon calendar](/full-moon-calendar) to see where the bright phase falls relative to the shower's peak, and plan to watch while the Moon is below the horizon if you can. The [next full moon](/full-moon) page shows when the Moon reaches full. For a darker sky, choose a night closer to new moon or a time when the Moon is below the horizon.

South of the equator, August is late winter and the full moon still rides high through long, cold nights. In either hemisphere, [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today) shows the daily illumination estimate and the Moon’s age so you can judge how much moonlight to expect tonight.
