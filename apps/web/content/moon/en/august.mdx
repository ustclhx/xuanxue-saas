## Where the name comes from

The Sturgeon Moon comes from the same family of almanac names that includes the Wolf, Worm and Harvest moons. North American almanacs, among them the Maine Farmers' Almanac and The Old Farmer's Almanac, gathered these names in the twentieth century from Native American, Colonial American and European sources.

Almanacs explain the August name through the lake sturgeon, a heavy, slow-growing fish that can live for many decades. In late summer it was widely caught in the Great Lakes and in waters such as Lake Champlain. A full moon named after the fish marked a time of year when it was a familiar sight along northern shorelines. The fish is easy to recognize, with rows of bony plates along its sides and whisker-like barbels under its snout, which helps explain why it stood out enough to lend its name to a month.

Alternative names lean toward fields rather than water. The Green Corn Moon refers to corn that is ripening but not yet ready for the main gathering, and the Grain Moon points to cereal crops coming in. Together the names show a season of transition: high summer is fading, and the heavier harvest work of autumn is close.

## Viewing tips

In August the full moon is still fairly low for northern observers, but it climbs a little higher than it did in June and July. That is because the Sun is slowly dropping from its summer peak, and the full moon always sits roughly opposite it. Expect moonrise in the east-southeast around sunset, with a modest arc across the southern sky.

Nights are lengthening, which gives you a longer window after dark. Warm, still evenings are pleasant for sitting outside, though summer haze can dull the contrast. Binoculars on a steady support show the dark lunar seas and bright rayed craters well; the days just before or after full are better for crater detail, because shadows near the terminator add depth. A small telescope at low power shows long shadow lines along that boundary each evening as the Moon waxes.

This month also brings the Perseid meteor shower. Check the [full moon calendar](/full-moon-calendar) to see where the bright phase falls relative to the shower's peak, and plan to watch while the Moon is below the horizon if you can. The [next full moon](/full-moon) page counts down to the moment of full, which helps you pick darker nights on either side.

South of the equator, August is late winter and the full moon still rides high through long, cold nights. In either hemisphere, [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today) shows the current illumination and the Moon's age so you can judge how much moonlight to expect tonight.
