## Where the name comes from

Flower Moon is one of the gentlest names in the almanac list, and one of the easiest to explain. When the Maine Farmers' Almanac in the 1930s and The Old Farmer's Almanac afterward published their twelve monthly names, they matched May with the flush of blossoms that covers meadows, roadsides and orchards in late spring.

The list drew on Native American, Colonial American and European folk names, and May had several to choose from. The Planting Moon recalls the stretch of the year when many farming communities historically put seed in the ground, and the Milk Moon, found in some European sources, is often linked to herds grazing on fresh spring pasture. These are records of how people organized their year, not guidance for today.

Since the name follows the calendar month, whichever full moon falls in May is the Flower Moon. If you want to know whether one is shining this evening, visit [full moon tonight](/full-moon).

## Viewing tips

May is a comfortable month for moon watching in the north. Evenings are mild, insects are not yet at their peak in many places, and the Flower Moon rises in the southeast near sunset. Because the high spring Sun pushes the full moon onto a lower nightly track, it never climbs very far above the southern horizon, which makes it easy to frame with trees or buildings.

That low position puts the full moon near the summer constellations. Look for Antares, a reddish star in Scorpius, somewhere in the same region; the contrast between its color and the pale Moon is noticeable even in a bright sky. A low moon also shows the moon illusion well, appearing larger beside the horizon than it does later when high, even though its size has not changed.

Nights are shortening, so the hours of true darkness are fewer. That matters if you want to see the Milky Way or faint stars: plan for the week around new moon, when the sky is darkest after evening twilight ends. For a complete list of upcoming full moons, see our [full moon dates](/full-moon-calendar).

Early risers have another option. The Eta Aquariid meteor shower is active in early May and is best seen before dawn, more so from the southern hemisphere. Its visibility depends heavily on how bright the Moon is that week, so check [moon phase today](/moon-phase-today) first. In the southern hemisphere, May is late autumn, and the full moon there rides high through the longer nights.
