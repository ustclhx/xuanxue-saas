## Where the name comes from

The Cold Moon closes the year in the list of full moon names popularized by North American almanacs, including the Maine Farmers' Almanac and The Old Farmer's Almanac. As with the other months, the almanacs collected names from Native American, Colonial American and European traditions and settled on one common label for each month.

The meaning is plain. December is when winter cold firmly arrives in much of North America and Europe. Ponds skin over with ice, the ground hardens, and early snow often stays on the ground. Naming the month's full moon after the cold gave a shared, easy reference point for the start of winter. Bright moonlit nights historically made winter travel and evening chores more manageable for rural households.

The alternative Long Night Moon looks at the calendar rather than the weather. The December full moon is always fairly near the winter solstice, the longest night of the year in the northern hemisphere. Older European calendars also used Moon before Yule, referring to the full moon that came ahead of the midwinter festival.

## Viewing tips

For northern observers, the December full moon is the high point of the year, literally. With the Sun at its lowest, the full moon opposite it follows a tall arc, rising in the northeast near sunset, climbing high in the south around midnight, and setting in the northwest near sunrise. It can be visible for nearly the whole night.

That height has practical benefits. The Moon is clear of trees and buildings for hours, and its light travels through less atmosphere when high, so it often looks bright white rather than orange. On snowy ground the moonlight can be strong enough to cast distinct shadows and let you read the landscape without a flashlight. On nights with thin, high cloud, look for a pale ring around the Moon; it is a halo, made as moonlight is bent by ice crystals in the cloud.

Cold nights call for a little preparation. Let binoculars or a telescope sit outside for a while so the optics adjust to the air temperature, and keep spare batteries warm in an inside pocket. The [next full moon](/full-moon) countdown tells you how long until the Cold Moon, and the [full moon calendar](/full-moon-calendar) helps you look ahead into the new year.

South of the equator, December is early summer, so the full moon there stays low and nights are short. The thin crescent around new moon is worth watching in either hemisphere, and [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today) shows exactly how much of the disk is lit right now.
