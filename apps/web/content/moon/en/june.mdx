## Where the name comes from

The Old Farmer’s Almanac records Strawberry Moon among Algonquin, Ojibwe, Dakota and Lakota names. It links the name to the early-summer strawberry season in what is now the northeastern United States. Wild strawberries ripen in a short window, which gives the name a particular place in the seasonal calendar.

A low June full moon can look amber or orange. That color comes from moonlight passing through more air near the horizon. It’s the same effect you can see during moonrise in any month.

The names vary from place to place. NASA lists Rose Moon in more than one naming tradition. Carver’s Hot Moon appears among names for lunar months, and The Old Farmer’s Almanac lists Berries Ripen Moon as a Haida name for this part of the year. ## Viewing tips

The June full moon usually takes a low arc across the southern sky at mid-northern latitudes because it is roughly opposite the high summer Sun. Farther north, in places like Scotland or southern Scandinavia, the full moon can pass low enough that buildings or hills block the view.

That low path has advantages. A low arc gives you more chances to frame the Moon against a skyline, bridge or hilltop. It also shines through a thick layer of air, often taking on a gold or amber tint, especially in humid or hazy weather. Use the [next full moon](/full-moon) page to find the phase date. For a view near the horizon, look up the moonrise time for your location.

Twilight is long in June, and in the far north the sky may never get fully dark. A full moon that rises shortly after sunset can appear in a sky that is still pale blue, which gives a soft, layered look to photos. Starting a few minutes before moonrise lets you see it emerge from the haze.

Evening crescents are harder to catch than in spring, because the Moon's path now meets the western horizon at a shallow angle and the thin crescent sits low in bright twilight. Waiting a few extra days until the Moon is near first quarter solves that. Follow the progression with [today's moon phase](/moon-phase-today). At mid-southern latitudes, June brings short days and a full moon that usually climbs high at night.
