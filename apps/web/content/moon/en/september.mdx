## Where the name comes from

Jonathan Carver recorded Corn Moon in his account of Indigenous lunar months, where it appears among the names for early fall. The Old Farmer’s Almanac uses Corn Moon for September when the Harvest Moon falls in October. The name recalls the season when corn was gathered.

The Harvest Moon works differently. It is not tied to a calendar month but to the September equinox: the Harvest Moon is whichever full moon falls closest to that equinox. In most years that is the September full moon, but every few years it lands in early October instead. The equinox rule is independent of the fixed month names shown in this calendar.

The name is associated with the extra evening light around the autumn equinox. Before electric lighting, farmers historically valued the bright evenings around the Harvest Moon, which let them keep bringing in crops after sunset. A full moon that rose soon after dusk several nights in a row gave them extra working light just when the harvest was heaviest. The Harvest Moon name has a long history in English and European farming traditions, older than the modern almanac lists.

## Viewing tips

The effect behind the Harvest Moon is something you can check for yourself. At mid-northern latitudes, the Moon usually rises noticeably later each night. Around the autumn equinox, though, its path meets the eastern horizon at a shallow angle, so the daily delay shrinks. Watch moonrise on the evenings just before and after full, and you may notice that moonrise comes only a little later from one night to the next. Jotting down moonrise times for four or five evenings in a row makes the pattern easy to see.

A rising full moon at this time of year often looks golden or orange, because its light crosses a long stretch of atmosphere near the horizon. It can also seem enormous, which is the moon illusion at work rather than any real change in size. Find a low eastern horizon over open fields or water for the best view.

In the southern hemisphere September brings the spring equinox, and the moonrise pattern is reversed: the delay between nights is longer than usual, and their own Harvest Moon effect comes around March and April instead.
