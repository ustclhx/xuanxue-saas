Card meanings follow the Rider–Waite–Smith tradition. For reference and self-reflection only; the cards do not predict the future or describe another person's feelings.

The five positions

  1. You. Where you stand in this relationship or question right now.
  2. How you see the other person. Your picture of them, which may differ from how they see themselves.
  3. What connects you. The common ground the question rests on.
  4. What needs attention. A tension or habit worth naming.
  5. A question to bring. Something to ask yourself, or to talk about together.

How to use this spread

The spread works for a partner, someone you are getting to know, or a relationship that has ended. It does not tell you what someone else feels or whether a relationship will last. For a shorter draw, the three-card spread covers past, present and future.

The four positions

  1. Where you are now. The situation as it stands, without the story around it.
  2. What you bring. A skill or habit you can rely on.
  3. What is in the way. An obstacle, inside or outside, worth looking at directly.
  4. A next step to consider. One concrete thing to try. The choice stays yours.

How to use this spread

Bring one specific question, such as a role you are weighing or a project that has stalled. The cards are a way to look at it from four sides. They are not career, legal or financial advice. To look up a card, see tarot card meanings.

The four positions

  1. What has come to light. Something that became clear over the past few weeks.
  2. What to set down. A worry, plan or habit that has run its course.
  3. What to keep. Something that is working and deserves care.
  4. A question for the next cycle. What to carry into the weeks until the next new moon.

Why the full moon

The full moon is the midpoint of the lunar month, and many traditions treat it as a time to look back before starting again. That is a custom for reflection; the phase of the Moon has no effect on the cards. See tonight's moon phase or the dates in the full moon calendar.
