Ganjiday · 干支日 · English guide

Chinese and Korean birth charts, explained in English

Ganjiday (干支日, “stem-and-branch day”) is a free set of tools for the traditional East Asian calendar arts. The calculators are written in Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese and Korean; this page explains what each one does and where to start.

Bazi — the Four Pillars of Destiny

八字 · 四柱

Bazi, literally “eight characters”, writes the moment of birth as four pillars – year, month, day and hour – each made of one Heavenly Stem and one Earthly Branch. Our calculator converts your birth date with the 24 solar terms and can apply true solar time.

Open the chart in Traditional Chinese: 八字排盤 (Bazi chart). The same tools are available in 简体中文 (Simplified Chinese).

Zi Wei Dou Shu — Purple Star Astrology

紫微斗數

Zi Wei Dou Shu arranges a chart into twelve palaces and places fourteen major stars in them from the lunar birth date and hour. Where Bazi weighs the balance of elements, Zi Wei asks which area of life each star sits in.

Open 紫微斗數排盤 (Zi Wei chart).

Saju — the Korean Four Pillars

사주 · 만세력

Saju is the Korean name for the same four-pillar system, and a manseryeok is the perpetual calendar used to look the pillars up. Korean practice usually corrects the birth hour to true solar time.

Open 만세력 (Manseryeok Saju chart, Korean).

Tarot

塔羅 · 타로

Tarot is not part of the Chinese calendar tradition, but many people use it next to a chart as a prompt for reflection. Cards are shuffled and drawn in your browser.

Open 三張牌塔羅 (three-card tarot).

How the charts are calculated

Charts are calculated in your browser with published calendar rules. No account or email address is needed for the free tools.
