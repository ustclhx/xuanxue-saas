The Sun upright meaning

Upright, The Sun is read as openness: things can be seen for what they are, and there is energy to enjoy them. In the Rider–Waite–Smith image a child rides out from a walled garden under a full sun, which readers take as confidence without pretence.

As a prompt, it asks where you can be plain about what is going well. It does not require you to be cheerful, and it does not cancel practical concerns that still deserve attention.

The Sun reversed meaning

Reversed, the same theme is read as dimmed or delayed: good news that is hard to take in, or enthusiasm that has run ahead of the facts. Ask what would make the situation clear enough to trust.

The Sun in love

In a relationship question, The Sun points to warmth that is easy to show and easy to receive. If you are single, it is a reminder that being straightforward about what you enjoy is more useful than guessing what someone wants to hear.

The Sun in career

At work the card is associated with visible results and credit that is shared. Use it to check whether the people who helped can see what their effort made possible.

Symbolism

  • The sun: clarity, with nothing left in shadow.
  • The child: confidence that has nothing to prove.
  • The wall and sunflowers: a protected place that has been outgrown.

Frequently asked questions

Does The Sun mean I must stay positive?

No. Its joy and clarity themes make room for honest feelings. You can appreciate a good moment without dismissing frustration, sadness, or practical concerns that also deserve attention.

What does The Sun reversed mean?

It is read as the same theme dimmed or delayed: confidence that is harder to reach, or optimism that needs checking against the facts.

Is The Sun a yes or a no?

Each card has one fixed answer for upright and one for reversed, published in our card list: The Sun is [Yes] upright and [answer] reversed. It is a prompt for reflection, not a prediction.
